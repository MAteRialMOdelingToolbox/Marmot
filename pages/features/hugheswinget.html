Hughes-Winget wrapper

A decorator that makes any small-strain (hypoelastic) material usable wherever a finite-strain material is expected – updated-Lagrangian elements such as C3D8UL, and the meshfree Displacement/... particles and material points.

Marmot::Materials::MaterialHypoElastic and Marmot::Materials::MaterialFiniteStrain are unrelated interfaces: the former returns the Cauchy stress and \(\partial\boldsymbol{\sigma}/\partial\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\) for a linearized strain increment, the latter the Kirchhoff stress and \(\partial\boldsymbol{\tau}/\partial\boldsymbol{F}\). HughesWingetWrapper bridges the two by integrating the small-strain model along an objective, co-rotational path.

Usage

The wrapper is registered per material under the name of the wrapped model, suffixed with /HUGHES-WINGET. In an EdelweissFE input file:

*material, name=VONMISES/HUGHES-WINGET, id=myMaterial
210000, 0.3, 550, 1000, 200, 1400

and in EdelweissMeshfree:

material = {"material": "VONMISES/HUGHES-WINGET", "properties": np.array([...])}

All material properties are forwarded to the wrapped model unchanged; the wrapper consumes none of them.

Registering a further material

Add one line to that material’s own <Name>Registration.cpp:

#include "Marmot/MarmotMaterialHughesWinget.h"
...
const static bool VonMisesHughesWingetIsRegistered = Marmot::Registry::MaterialFiniteStrainFactory::registerMaterial<
  HughesWingetWrapper< VonMisesModel > >( "VONMISES/HUGHES-WINGET" );

Theory

Let \(\boldsymbol{F}^{(n)}\) and \(\boldsymbol{F}^{(n+1)}\) be the deformation gradients at the beginning and the end of the increment. The velocity gradient increment is evaluated on the mid-step configuration,

\[\Delta\boldsymbol{l} = \left(\boldsymbol{F}^{(n+1)}-\boldsymbol{F}^{(n)}\right)\boldsymbol{F}_\mathrm{mid}^{-1}, \qquad \boldsymbol{F}_\mathrm{mid} = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(\boldsymbol{F}^{(n)}+\boldsymbol{F}^{(n+1)}\right),\]

and split into its symmetric and skew parts

\[\Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(\Delta\boldsymbol{l}+\Delta\boldsymbol{l}^{T}\right), \qquad \Delta\boldsymbol{\Omega} = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(\Delta\boldsymbol{l}-\Delta\boldsymbol{l}^{T}\right).\]

The incremental rotation follows from the Cayley transform

\[\Delta\boldsymbol{R} = \left(\boldsymbol{I}-\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta\boldsymbol{\Omega}\right)^{-1} \left(\boldsymbol{I}+\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta\boldsymbol{\Omega}\right),\]

which is orthogonal to machine precision. The stress carried over from the previous increment is rotated forward,

\[\boldsymbol{\sigma}_\mathrm{rot} = \Delta\boldsymbol{R}\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(n)}\,\Delta\boldsymbol{R}^{T},\]

the wrapped material is evaluated with \(\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_\mathrm{rot}, \Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\right)\), and its updated Cauchy stress is pushed forward to the Kirchhoff stress expected by the consumers,

\[\boldsymbol{\tau} = J\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(n+1)}, \qquad J = \det\boldsymbol{F}^{(n+1)}.\]

Superimposed rigid rotation is reproduced exactly, in a single increment and for any rotation angle below \(180^\circ\): for \(\boldsymbol{F}^{(n+1)}=\boldsymbol{Q}\boldsymbol{F}^{(n)}\) the increment \(\Delta\boldsymbol{l}\) is exactly skew, hence \(\Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}=\boldsymbol{0}\) and \(\Delta\boldsymbol{R}=\boldsymbol{Q}\).

Algorithmic tangent

With \(\boldsymbol{M}=\boldsymbol{F}_\mathrm{mid}^{-1}\), \(\boldsymbol{P}=\boldsymbol{I}-\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta\boldsymbol{l}\), \(\boldsymbol{A}=\boldsymbol{I}-\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta\boldsymbol{\Omega}\) and \(\boldsymbol{G}=\left(\boldsymbol{I}+\Delta\boldsymbol{R}\right)\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(n)}\Delta\boldsymbol{R}^{T}\),

\[\frac{\partial\Delta l_{ij}}{\partial F_{kl}} = P_{ik}M_{lj}, \qquad \frac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{rot},ij}}{\partial\Delta\Omega_{kl}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(A^{-1}_{ik}G_{lj}+A^{-1}_{jk}G_{li}\right),\]

from which, with \(\mathbf{C}\) the tangent reported by the wrapped material,

\[\frac{\partial\tau_{ij}}{\partial F_{kl}} = \sigma^{(n+1)}_{ij}\,J\,F^{-1}_{lk} + J\left(\frac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{rot},ij}}{\partial\Delta\Omega_{mn}} \frac{\partial\Delta\Omega_{mn}}{\partial F_{kl}} + C_{ijmn}\frac{\partial\Delta\varepsilon_{mn}}{\partial F_{kl}}\right).\]

Tangent modes

The second template parameter selects how the tangent is obtained.

Mode

Wrapped-material evaluations

Consistency

Analytic (default)

1

Exact while the wrapped model responds elastically. The expression above implicitly assumes \(\partial\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(n+1)}/\partial\boldsymbol{\sigma}_\mathrm{rot}=\boldsymbol{I}\), so during plastic flow the relative tangent error is bounded by roughly \(\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}\|/\|\mathbf{C}^\mathrm{e}\|\).

Exact

7

Recovers \(\boldsymbol{S}=\partial\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(n+1)}/\partial\boldsymbol{\sigma}_\mathrm{rot}\) by forward-differencing the six incoming stress components. Fully consistent. Worth the cost for softening models under large incremental rotation.

Numerical

11

Forward-differences the complete update with respect to \(\boldsymbol{F}\). Fully consistent and model-agnostic; intended for verification and debugging.

Characteristic element length

The wrapper forwards setCharacteristicElementLength to the wrapped model, but derives no length from geometry. Until one is assigned the wrapped model sees NaN, so a model that actually depends on it (for a mesh-adjusted softening modulus) fails loudly rather than silently regularising against a wrong length. Models that never read it are unaffected.

In EdelweissMeshfree, assign one per particle – particles carry no mesh, so nothing else can:

for particle in model.particles.values():
    particle.setProperty("characteristic element length",
                         particle.getVolumeUndeformed() ** (1.0 / dimension))

Warning

Only the stress is rotated. Tensor-valued internal variables of the wrapped model – kinematic-hardening back stresses, plastic strain tensors, anisotropic damage tensors – are passed through untouched and are therefore not objective under large incremental rotation. Models whose internal state is scalar or isotropic-invariant (isotropic hardening, scalar damage) are unaffected.

Decorator presenting a small-strain (hypoelastic) material as a finite-strain material.

The wrapped material is driven by the objective strain increment of the Hughes-Winget algorithm, evaluated on the mid-step configuration,

\[ \Delta\boldsymbol{l} = \left(\boldsymbol{F}^{(n+1)} - \boldsymbol{F}^{(n)}\right) \boldsymbol{F}_{\text{mid}}^{-1}, \qquad \boldsymbol{F}_{\text{mid}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(\boldsymbol{F}^{(n)} + \boldsymbol{F}^{(n+1)}\right), \]
split into its symmetric and skew parts \( \Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \) and \( \Delta\boldsymbol{\Omega} \). The incremental rotation follows from the Cayley transform
\[ \Delta\boldsymbol{R} = \left(\boldsymbol{I} - \tfrac{1}{2}\Delta\boldsymbol{\Omega}\right)^{-1} \left(\boldsymbol{I} + \tfrac{1}{2}\Delta\boldsymbol{\Omega}\right), \]
which is orthogonal to machine precision. The stress carried over from the previous increment is rotated forward, \( \boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\text{rot}} = \Delta\boldsymbol{R}\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(n)} \Delta\boldsymbol{R}^{T} \), the wrapped material is evaluated, and the resulting Cauchy stress is pushed to the Kirchhoff stress \( \boldsymbol{\tau} = J\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(n+1)} \) expected by the finite-strain consumers.

Note

Only the stress is rotated. Tensor-valued internal variables of the wrapped material (kinematic hardening back stresses, plastic strain tensors, anisotropic damage tensors) are passed through untouched and are therefore not objective under large incremental rotations. Models whose internal state is scalar or isotropic-invariant are unaffected.

Note

The characteristic element length is not derived from any geometry here. It must be assigned explicitly via setCharacteristicElementLength; until then it is NaN, so a wrapped material that relies on it fails loudly instead of silently regularising incorrectly.

Template Parameters:

  • BaseMaterialType – A concrete class derived from Marmot::Materials::MaterialHypoElastic.

  • tangentMode – How the algorithmic tangent is evaluated, see Marmot::Materials::HughesWingetTangent.
