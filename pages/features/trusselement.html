Truss Element

Theory

A truss (bar) element in two or three spatial dimensions with 2 (linear) or 3 (quadratic; node order end, end, mid) nodes. It carries only an axial force, e.g., as a reinforcement bar embedded in continuum elements. The material sees a uniaxial stress state along the truss axis, found by the uniaxial stress reductions of the material base classes. The names follow Marmot’s scheme T<dimension>D<nodes> (Abaqus: T2D2, T3D2 etc. as well); the suffix CR marks the finite strain variants.

Element and material go in pairs:

element

kinematics

materials

T2D2, T2D3, T3D2, T3D3

geometrically linear

hypoelastic, e.g., LINEARELASTIC, VONMISES

T2D2CR, T2D3CR, T3D2CR, T3D3CR

finite strain

finite strain materials whose response to a diagonal deformation gradient is diagonal, e.g., the isotropic COMPRESSIBLENEOHOOKE, FINITESTRAINJ2PLASTICITY, VONMISES/HUGHES-WINGET

A material of the other kind is rejected with a hint to the matching element (VONMISES is a hypoelastic material; its finite strain counterpart is VONMISES/HUGHES-WINGET). The uniaxial reduction of finite strain materials condenses only \(\tau_{22}\) and \(\tau_{33}\); a material that answers a diagonal deformation gradient with shear stresses (e.g., an anisotropic one, its axes read in the truss frame) is rejected instead of having its shear stresses dropped.

Small strain (T2D2, T2D3, T3D2, T3D3): the axial strain is \(\varepsilon = \mathbf{T} \cdot \partial \mathbf{u} / \partial S\) with the reference unit tangent \(\mathbf{T}\) and arc length \(S\). The material is called through Marmot::Materials::MaterialHypoElastic::computeUniaxialStress. As any geometrically linear element – and unlike T2D2 in Abaqus with NLGEOM – the element strains under a finite rotation: use the CR variant whenever rotations are not small.

Formulation of the finite strain variants (T2D2CR, T2D3CR, T3D2CR, T3D3CR): total Lagrangian; at each quadrature point the material is evaluated in the frame co-rotated with the bar’s current tangent \(\mathbf{g} = \partial \mathbf{x} / \partial S\), \(\mathbf{F} = \mathrm{diag}(\lambda, \lambda_2, \lambda_3)\) with the axial stretch \(\lambda = |\mathbf{g}|\) and the lateral stretches found by Marmot::Materials::MaterialFiniteStrain::computeUniaxialStress such that \(\tau_{22} = \tau_{33} = 0\). Rigid body rotations are exactly stress free. For 2 nodes the frame is the chord, and the element is the co-rotational truss of Crisfield (Non-linear Finite Element Analysis of Solids and Structures, Vol. 1, ch. 3), hence CR; for 3 nodes the frame follows the curved bar point by point. With the first Piola-Kirchhoff stress \(P = \tau_{11} / \lambda\) and the current axis \(\mathbf{n}\),

\[\mathbf{f}_a = \int P\, \mathbf{n}\, \frac{\partial N_a}{\partial S}\, A_0\, \mathrm{d}S, \qquad \mathbf{K}_{ab} = \int \Big[ \underbrace{\frac{\mathrm{d}P}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\, \mathbf{n} \otimes \mathbf{n}}_{\text{material}} + \underbrace{\frac{P}{\lambda} (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{n} \otimes \mathbf{n})}_{\text{geometric}} \Big] \frac{\partial N_a}{\partial S} \frac{\partial N_b}{\partial S}\, A_0\, \mathrm{d}S :\]

the material stiffness along the bar and the geometric (initial stress) stiffness transverse to it, with \(P / \lambda = S_{11}\), the second Piola-Kirchhoff stress – the term behind the snap-through of a shallow truss.

Element property: the cross section area \(A_0\) (in the units of the model, e.g., mm² for N, mm, MPa).

Quadrature: Gauss, exact to degree \(2p - 1\) (1 point for 2 nodes, 2 points for 3 nodes); exact for the stiffness of straight trusses with a linear elastic material, approximate for curved 3-node bars.

Quadrature point states: stress (axial Cauchy stress), strain (small strain: axial strain; finite strain: \(\ln\lambda\)), normal force, elastic energy, dissipation; for the CR variants also lateral stretches and kirchhoff stress; followed by the material state.

Limitations

  • A truss has no lateral stiffness. A node connected only to collinear small-strain trusses (or to CR trusses without tension) is free laterally, and the stiffness matrix is singular – the classic trap of a first truss model. Restrain such nodes, e.g., by boundary conditions, by embedding the truss, or by pre-tension.

  • A quadratic truss (T2D3, T3D3 and their CR variants) has internal zero-energy modes of its mid node transverse to the axis: one in 2D, two in 3D. Harmless when the truss is embedded in a continuum (the main use); stand-alone, the mid node must be restrained transversally (constraint, boundary condition) or pre-tensioned.

  • Only axial forces: no bending, no distributed (surface) loads.

How to check

A hand calculation for a first model: an inclined linear elastic bar of length \(L\) and area \(A\), one end fixed, the other pulled along the bar by \(\Delta\), carries the normal force \(N = E A \Delta / L\) and the end forces \(\pm N \mathbf{T}\); the stiffness along the bar is \(EA/L\), the one transverse to it zero (small strain) or \(N / L\) (CR, geometric stiffness). The EdelweissFE test tests/test_trusses.py::test_truss_small_strain_inclined is this check as a complete model.

Implementation

template<int nDim, int nNodes>
class TrussElement : public Marmot::Discretizations::Elements::Element

Truss (bar) element in two or three spatial dimensions, for small and finite strains.

The truss carries only an axial force. The material sees a uniaxial stress state along the truss axis, found by the uniaxial stress reductions of the material base classes. Element and material go in pairs:

  • T<d>D<n> (SmallStrain, geometrically linear): hypoelastic materials (Marmot::Materials::MaterialHypoElastic), e.g. LINEARELASTIC, VONMISES;

  • T<d>D<n>CR (FiniteStrain): finite strain materials (Marmot::Materials::MaterialFiniteStrain) whose response to a diagonal deformation gradient is diagonal, e.g. the isotropic COMPRESSIBLENEOHOOKE, FINITESTRAINJ2PLASTICITY, VONMISES/HUGHES-WINGET.

  • SmallStrain: the axial strain is \(\varepsilon = \boldsymbol{T} \cdot \frac{\partial \boldsymbol{u}} {\partial S}\) with the reference unit tangent \(\boldsymbol{T}\) and the reference arc length \(S\). As any geometrically linear element (unlike T2D2 in Abaqus with NLGEOM), a finite rotation strains the truss: use the CR variant whenever rotations are not small.

  • FiniteStrain (total Lagrangian): the material is evaluated in the frame co-rotated with the bar’s current tangent at the quadrature point, \(\boldsymbol{F} = \mathrm{diag}( \lambda, \lambda_2, \lambda_3 )\) with the axial stretch \(\lambda = |\boldsymbol{g}|\) of the current tangent \(\boldsymbol{g} = \partial \boldsymbol{x} / \partial S\), and the lateral stretches \(\lambda_2, \lambda_3\) found such that the lateral stresses vanish. Rigid body rotations are therefore exactly stress free. For 2 nodes the frame is the chord and the element is the co-rotational truss (Crisfield, Non-linear Finite Element Analysis of Solids and Structures, Vol. 1, ch. 3), hence “CR”; for 3 nodes the frame follows the curved bar point by point. The internal forces follow from the nominal axial stress \(P = \tau_{11} / \lambda\) (the first Piola-Kirchhoff stress) and the current axis \(\boldsymbol{n}\), \(\boldsymbol{f}_a = \int P \, \boldsymbol{n} \frac{\partial N_a} {\partial S} A_0 \, \mathrm{d}S\), with the consistent tangent \(\boldsymbol{K}_{ab} = \int \left[ \frac{\mathrm{d} P}{\mathrm{d}\lambda} \boldsymbol{n} \otimes \boldsymbol{n} + \frac{P}{\lambda} ( \boldsymbol{I} - \boldsymbol{n} \otimes \boldsymbol{n} ) \right] \frac{\partial N_a}{\partial S} \frac{\partial N_b}{\partial S} A_0 \, \mathrm{d}S\): the first term is the material stiffness along the bar, the second the geometric (initial stress) stiffness transverse to it, with \(P / \lambda = S_{11}\) the second Piola-Kirchhoff stress – the term behind the snap-through of a shallow truss.

Element property: the (reference) cross section area \(A_0\).

Quadrature: Gauss rule exact to degree \(2p - 1\) (1 point for 2 nodes, 2 points for 3 nodes), exact for the stiffness of straight trusses with a linear elastic material.

Limitations: a truss has no lateral stiffness. A node connected only to collinear small-strain trusses (or to CR trusses without tension) is free laterally and the stiffness matrix is singular; a quadratic truss additionally has internal zero-energy modes of its mid node transverse to the axis (one in 2D, two in 3D). Restrain them by embedding, constraints, boundary conditions or pre-tension.

Quadrature point states: stress (axial Cauchy stress), strain (small strain: axial strain; finite strain: logarithmic axial strain \(\ln\lambda\)), normal force, elastic energy, dissipation (both integrated over the quadrature point’s volume), lateral stretches and kirchhoff stress (finite strain only), followed by the material state.

Template Parameters:

  • nDim – number of spatial dimensions, 2 or 3

  • nNodes – number of nodes, 2 (linear) or 3 (quadratic, node order: end, end, mid)

inline virtual void computeCriticalTimeStepForExplicitDynamics(double &criticalTimeStep, const double *QTotal) override

The critical time step of explicit dynamics: the stiffest axial mode of the bar with the row-sum lumped mass (computeLumpedInertia).

With the reference length \(L\) and the wave speed \(c\) of the material: 2 nodes, masses \(\rho A L (1/2, 1/2)\), \(\omega_{\max}^2 = 4 c^2 / L^2\), \(\Delta t = 2 / \omega_{\max} = L / c\); 3 nodes, masses \(\rho A L (1/6, 1/6, 2/3)\), \(\omega_{\max}^2 = 24 c^2 / L^2\), \(\Delta t = L / (\sqrt{6} c)\). The wave speed is the materials’ maximum (P-wave) one, larger than the bar wave speed \(\sqrt{E/\rho}\): the estimate is on the safe side.

Parameters:

  • criticalTimeStep – [out] The critical time step.

  • QTotal – [in] The total nodal displacements (the finite strain materials are evaluated at the current stretch).
