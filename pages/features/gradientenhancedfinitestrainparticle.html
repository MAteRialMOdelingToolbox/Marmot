Gradient-Enhanced Finite-Strain Particles

Theory

The gradient-enhanced finite-strain particles are the RKPM particles of the implicit-gradient finite-strain formulation. Each particle couples the displacement field \(\boldsymbol u\) to a single scalar nonlocal field \(\bar N\) (nodal fields displacement and nonlocal damage, dofs ordered node by node as \(\{u_1,\dots,u_{n_\mathrm{dim}},\bar N\}\)), owns one Gradient-Enhanced Finite-Strain Material Point and is integrated at that single point (nodal integration). The material is any material of the interface Marmot::Materials::MaterialGradientEnhancedFiniteStrain, e.g. Gradient-enhanced compressible Neo-Hooke damage or Gradient-enhanced finite-strain Drucker-Prager. The shape functions come from the reproducing kernel approximation (Meshfree Approximation); the particle interface, the integration schemes and the corrections are introduced in Meshfree Particles. The weak forms are those of Gradient-Enhanced Finite-Strain Displacement Element, in increment form.

There are three particle classes:

  • GradientEnhancedFiniteStrainParticle: a point particle, direct nodal integration with the point values of the shape function gradients;

  • GradientEnhancedFiniteStrainParticleSQCNI: a quadrilateral or hexahedral smoothing domain with smoothed gradients (SQCNI / SNNI) and faces for distributed loads;

  • GradientEnhancedFiniteStrainParticleSQCNIxNSNI: the SQCNI particle plus the NSNI stabilization.

Kinematics

The dofs are the increments \(\Delta\boldsymbol q\) of the current step. The shape functions \(N_B\) and their gradients \(\partial N_B/\partial\boldsymbol Y\) are evaluated in the intermediate reference configuration \(\boldsymbol Y\) of the last accepted state, when the host assigns the kernel functions. They give

\[\Delta\boldsymbol u = N_B\,\Delta\boldsymbol q^U_B, \qquad \Delta F_{ij} = \delta_{ij} + \Delta q^U_{Bi}\,\frac{\partial N_B}{\partial Y_j}, \qquad \Delta\bar N = N_B\,\Delta q^N_B, \qquad \frac{\partial\Delta\bar N}{\partial Y_j} = \Delta q^N_B\,\frac{\partial N_B}{\partial Y_j},\]

and the material point is evaluated at \(\boldsymbol F = \Delta\boldsymbol F\,\boldsymbol F_n\). Spatial and undeformed gradients follow from \(\partial(\cdot)/\partial x_i = \partial(\cdot)/\partial Y_j\,\Delta F^{-1}_{ji}\) and \(\partial(\cdot)/\partial X_i = \partial(\cdot)/\partial Y_j\,F_{n,ji}\). On acceptance, the material point state is updated, the intermediate reference configuration moves (center, volume \(V_Y = V_0\det\boldsymbol F_n\), smoothing domain); the shape functions are re-evaluated when the host assigns the kernel functions again.

Weak forms

With the test functions \(T_A\) (equal to \(N_A\), except that VCI corrects their gradients), the undeformed particle volume \(V_0\) and \(c = R^2\) from the material, the residuals per node \(A\) are

\[\begin{split}r^U_{Aj} &= \frac{\partial T_A}{\partial x_i}\,\tau_{ij}\,V_0 + \rho_0\,a_j\,T_A\,V_0 ,\\ r^N_A &= \Bigl( T_A\,\Delta\bar N + c\,\frac{\partial T_A}{\partial X_i}\,\frac{\partial\Delta\bar N}{\partial X_i} - T_A\,\Delta L \Bigr) V_0 ,\end{split}\]

i.e. the Helmholtz equation \(\bar N - c\,\nabla_X^2\bar N = L\) is solved in the undeformed configuration for the increment of the nonlocal field, with the change \(\Delta L\) of the local driving force as its source. The acceleration \(\boldsymbol a\) follows from the Newmark-beta update of \(\Delta\boldsymbol u\) with the particle properties newmark-beta beta and newmark-beta gamma; \(\beta = 0\) switches the inertia off and the particle is at rest (\(\boldsymbol v = \boldsymbol a = \boldsymbol 0\)). A zero-length increment adds no inertia: \(\boldsymbol v\) and \(\boldsymbol a\) are left as they are, and the inertia tangent is zero (see Displacement Particle).

Tangent

With the tangents of the material point with respect to \(\Delta\boldsymbol F\) and \(\bar N\),

\[\begin{split}K^{UU}_{AjBk} &= \Bigl( \frac{\partial T_A}{\partial x_i}\,\frac{\partial\tau_{ij}}{\partial\Delta F_{kL}}\, \frac{\partial N_B}{\partial Y_L} - \frac{\partial T_A}{\partial x_k}\,\tau_{ij}\,\frac{\partial N_B}{\partial x_i} + \rho_0\,\frac{\partial a_j}{\partial\Delta u_k}\,T_A N_B \Bigr) V_0 ,\\ K^{UN}_{AjB} &= \frac{\partial T_A}{\partial x_i}\,\frac{\partial\tau_{ij}}{\partial\bar N}\,N_B\,V_0 ,\\ K^{NU}_{ABk} &= -T_A\,\frac{\partial L}{\partial\Delta F_{kL}}\,\frac{\partial N_B}{\partial Y_L}\,V_0 ,\\ K^{NN}_{AB} &= \Bigl( T_A N_B\,\bigl(1 - \frac{\partial L}{\partial\bar N}\bigr) + c\,\frac{\partial T_A}{\partial X_i}\,\frac{\partial N_B}{\partial X_i} \Bigr) V_0 ,\end{split}\]

as in Gradient-Enhanced Finite-Strain Cell and Gradient-Enhanced Finite-Strain Displacement Element.

Integration schemes

Point particle. The gradients \(\partial N_B/\partial\boldsymbol Y\) are the point values of the meshfree approximation at the particle center. The point particle has no faces, so it supports no distributed loads (its computeDistributedLoad throws); the SQCNI / NSNI particles support PRESSURE and CWFCORRECTION.

BODYFORCE (all gradient-enhanced particles) is a dead load per unit undeformed volume on the displacement field, \(P_{Ai} \mathrel{-}= T_A\,b_i\,V_0\), with the host’s sign convention for external loads; it has no tangent and does not load the nonlocal field.

SQCNI / SNNI. The particle is a quadrilateral (2D) or hexahedral (3D) smoothing domain \(\Omega_Y\) with the material point at its centroid. As in stabilized conforming nodal integration (Chen et al., 2001), the gradients are replaced by their average over the smoothing domain, turned into a boundary integral and evaluated with one point per face \(f\) (the face center \(\boldsymbol Y_f\)),

\[\frac{\partial N_B}{\partial Y_i} \approx \frac{1}{V_{\Omega_Y}} \sum_f N_B(\boldsymbol Y_f)\,n_i\,dA_f ,\]

while \(N_B\) is the point value at the center. The smoothed gradients are used for both fields, i.e. also for \(\nabla\Delta\bar N\) and the Laplacian term of the nonlocal balance. The smoothing domain is stored by its vertex displacements (state vertex displacements) and is updated at each accepted increment from \(\boldsymbol F_n\) of the material point, according to the smoothing domain update type:

NSNI. Nodal integration evaluates the integrand at one point only, which leaves spurious zero-energy modes. The naturally stabilized nodal integration (NSNI) expands the integrand to first order about the particle center and keeps the second-order term of the integral, with the second moments \(\boldsymbol M^Y\) of the particle domain about its centroid. The second derivatives of the shape functions are smoothed like the first ones, from the gradients at the face centers (symmetrized),

\[\frac{\partial^2 N_B}{\partial Y_I\,\partial Y_J} \approx \frac{1}{2V_{\Omega_Y}} \sum_f \Bigl( \frac{\partial N_B}{\partial Y_I}(\boldsymbol Y_f)\,n_J + \frac{\partial N_B}{\partial Y_J}(\boldsymbol Y_f)\,n_I \Bigr) dA_f ,\]

the Kirchhoff stress gradient is linearized as

\[\frac{\partial\tau_{ij}}{\partial Y_K} \approx \frac{\partial\tau_{ij}}{\partial\Delta F_{mM}}\, \Delta q^U_{Bm}\,\frac{\partial^2 N_B}{\partial Y_M\,\partial Y_K},\]

and the momentum residual receives

\[r^{U,\mathrm{stab}}_{Aj} = \Delta F^{-1}_{mi}\,\frac{\partial^2 N_A}{\partial Y_m\,\partial Y_L}\, \frac{M^Y_{LK}}{J_Y}\,\frac{\partial\tau_{ij}}{\partial Y_K}, \qquad \boldsymbol M^Y = J_Y\,\boldsymbol F_n\,\boldsymbol M^0\,\boldsymbol F_n^\mathsf{T},\quad J_Y = \det\boldsymbol F_n,\]

with the second moments \(\boldsymbol M^0\) of the undeformed cell (computed at construction; \(\boldsymbol M^Y\) is initialized to \(\boldsymbol M^0\) and pushed forward at each accepted increment). The stabilization acts on the momentum balance only; the stress gradient does not contain the contribution \(\partial\boldsymbol\tau/\partial\bar N\,\nabla_Y\Delta\bar N\) of the nonlocal field, and the second derivatives are not corrected by VCI.

Note

The tangent of the NSNI stabilization is approximate by construction. It differentiates the second derivatives of the displacement increment and \(\Delta\boldsymbol F^{-1}\), but not \(\partial\boldsymbol\tau/\partial \Delta\boldsymbol F\) itself: \(\partial^2\boldsymbol\tau/\partial\boldsymbol F^2\) is not exposed by the material interface, and the dependence of \(\partial\boldsymbol\tau/\partial\Delta\boldsymbol F\) on \(\bar N\) (the damage) is omitted, so the U-N block has no stabilization contribution at all. The rows of the nonlocal balance are exact. The module test bounds the error of the U-U block (below \(5\cdot10^{-3}\)) and does not check the U-N block for the NSNI particles, whose error in a damaged state is not small.

Corrections

VCI. All particles implement the hooks of variationally consistent integration (Chen, Hillman, Rüter, 2013): the host assembles the integration constraints from the particles’ boundary and domain terms and returns correction coefficients \(\eta_{AiC}\), with which the test function gradients are corrected, \(\partial T_A/\partial Y_i \mathrel{+}= \eta_{AiC}\,P_C(\boldsymbol Y)\), with a monomial basis \(P_C\) of order VCI order at the particle center. The corrected test gradients enter both weak forms. For the SQCNI particles, the boundary term is evaluated at the face centers with the face surface vectors of the intermediate configuration.

CWF. The distributed load CWFCORRECTION (SQCNI particles, per face) adds the boundary term of the momentum weak form on a face, which the smoothed gradients otherwise leave unbalanced. With the surface vector \(\boldsymbol N\,dA_Y\) and the center \(\boldsymbol Y_f\) of the face in the intermediate configuration,

\[\boldsymbol t = \boldsymbol\tau\,\Delta\boldsymbol F^{-\mathsf T}\,\frac{\boldsymbol N\,dA_Y}{J_Y}, \qquad f_{Ai} \mathrel{-}= T_A(\boldsymbol Y_f)\,t_i ,\]

where the division by \(J_Y\) accounts for the internal force being integrated over the undeformed volume \(V_0 = V_Y/J_Y\). The tangent contains the geometric part from \(\Delta\boldsymbol F^{-\mathsf T}\), the material part from \(\partial\boldsymbol\tau/\partial\Delta\boldsymbol F\) and the coupling \(\partial\boldsymbol\tau/\partial\bar N\) to the nonlocal dofs. For a homogeneous deformation, the corrections on all faces cancel the displacement residual (exactly so after deformation only for the DeformationGradient update, whose smoothing domain coincides with the particle).

Pressure. PRESSURE (SQCNI particles, per face, load[0] = \(p\)) is a follower load, \(\boldsymbol f = -\Delta J\,\Delta\boldsymbol F^{-\mathsf T}\,p\,\boldsymbol N\,dA_Y\), \(f_{Ai} \mathrel{-}= T_A(\boldsymbol Y_f)\,f_i\), with its load stiffness. As for the finite elements, a positive pressure pushes on the face (against the outward normal). For DeformationGradient the face geometry is that of the current smoothing domain; for the other update types the undeformed surface vector is mapped by Nanson’s formula with \(\boldsymbol F_n\), and the undeformed face center is translated by the particle displacement. Neither load acts on the nonlocal field.

Nodally integrated point particle for gradient-enhanced (implicit-gradient) finite-strain materials without a micropolar continuum.

The particle couples the displacement field \( \boldsymbol{u} \) to a single scalar nonlocal field \( \bar{N} \) (nodal fields displacement and nonlocal damage, node block \( n_\mathrm{dim}+1 \), dofs ordered node by node as \( \{u_1,\dots,u_{n_\mathrm{dim}},\bar{N}\} \)). It owns a single Marmot::Discretizations::MaterialPoints::GradientEnhancedFiniteStrainMaterialPoint, which drives a Marmot::Materials::MaterialGradientEnhancedFiniteStrain, and is integrated with that single point (direct nodal integration).

Kinematics. The dofs are the increments of the current step. The trial shape functions \( N_B \) and gradients \( \partial N_B/\partial\boldsymbol{Y} \) are those of the meshfree approximation at the particle center in the intermediate reference configuration \( \boldsymbol{Y} \) (the last accepted configuration), evaluated in assignMeshfreeKernelFunctions(). They give

\[ \Delta\boldsymbol{u} = N_B\,\Delta\boldsymbol{q}^U_B,\quad \Delta F_{ij} = \delta_{ij} + \Delta q^U_{Bi}\,\frac{\partial N_B}{\partial Y_j},\quad \Delta\bar{N} = N_B\,\Delta q^N_B,\quad \frac{\partial\Delta\bar{N}}{\partial Y_j} = \Delta q^N_B\,\frac{\partial N_B}{\partial Y_j}, \]
and the material point is evaluated at \( \boldsymbol{F} = \Delta\boldsymbol{F}\,\boldsymbol{F}_n \).

Weak forms. With the test functions \( T_A \) (identical to \( N_A \), except that VCI corrects their gradients), the undeformed particle volume \( V_0 \), \( c = R^2 \) from the material and \( \partial(\cdot)/\partial x_i = \partial(\cdot)/\partial Y_j\,\Delta F^{-1}_{ji} \), \( \partial(\cdot)/\partial X_i = \partial(\cdot)/\partial Y_j\,F_{n,ji} \), the residuals per node A are

\[ r^U_{Aj} = \frac{\partial T_A}{\partial x_i}\,\tau_{ij}\,V_0 + \rho_0\,a_j\,T_A\,V_0 ,\qquad r^N_A = \Bigl( T_A\,\Delta\bar{N} + c\,\frac{\partial T_A}{\partial X_i}\frac{\partial\Delta\bar{N}}{\partial X_i} - T_A\,\Delta L \Bigr) V_0 , \]
i.e. the Helmholtz equation \( \bar{N} - c\,\nabla_X^2\bar{N} = L \) is solved in the undeformed configuration for the INCREMENT of the nonlocal field, with the change \( \Delta L \) of the local driving force (GradientEnhancedFiniteStrainMaterialPoint::response) as its source: the nodal values of the reproducing kernels carry only the increment of the step, and the kernels are rebuilt on the moved nodes every increment, so the total form \( T_A(\bar{N} - L) + c\,\nabla_X T_A\cdot\nabla_X\bar{N} \) would need \( \nabla_X\bar{N} \) as an accumulated state of the particle (tested: it changed the nonlocal field by less than the step dependence of the kinematics, without bringing it closer to the result of a single increment). The acceleration \( \boldsymbol{a} \) follows from the Newmark-beta update of \( \Delta\boldsymbol{u} \) ( \( \beta = 0 \) switches the inertia off).

Tangent. With \( \partial\boldsymbol{\tau}/\partial\Delta\boldsymbol{F} \) etc. from the material point,

\[ K^{UU}_{AjBk} = \Bigl( \frac{\partial T_A}{\partial x_i}\frac{\partial\tau_{ij}}{\partial\Delta F_{kL}} \frac{\partial N_B}{\partial Y_L} - \frac{\partial T_A}{\partial x_k}\,\tau_{ij}\, \frac{\partial N_B}{\partial x_i} + \rho_0\,\frac{\partial a_j}{\partial\Delta u_k}\,T_A N_B \Bigr) V_0 ,\quad K^{UN}_{AjB} = \frac{\partial T_A}{\partial x_i}\frac{\partial\tau_{ij}}{\partial\bar{N}}\,N_B\,V_0 , \]
\[ K^{NU}_{ABk} = -T_A\,\frac{\partial L}{\partial\Delta F_{kL}}\frac{\partial N_B}{\partial Y_L}\,V_0 ,\qquad K^{NN}_{AB} = \Bigl( T_A N_B \bigl( 1 - \frac{\partial L}{\partial\bar{N}} \bigr) + c\,\frac{\partial T_A}{\partial X_i}\frac{\partial N_B}{\partial X_i} \Bigr) V_0 , \]
as in the MPM cell and the finite element.

VCI. The particle implements the variationally consistent integration hooks of Particle: the test function gradients are corrected by \( \partial T_A/\partial Y_i \mathrel{+}= \eta_{AiC}\,P_C(\boldsymbol{Y}) \) with a monomial basis \( P_C \) of order VCI order (Chen, Hillman, Rüter, 2013). The correction applies to both weak forms.

The point particle has no faces and supports no distributed loads; use GradientEnhancedFiniteStrainParticleSQCNI for boundary loads. computeBodyLoad() applies a body force per unit undeformed volume, also for the derived particles.

inline virtual void acceptStateAndPosition() override

Accept the increment and move the intermediate reference configuration.

class GradientEnhancedFiniteStrainParticleSQCNI : public Marmot::Discretizations::Meshfree::GradientEnhancedFiniteStrainParticle<nDim>

Inheritance diagram for Marmot::Discretizations::Meshfree::GradientEnhancedFiniteStrainParticleSQCNI:

Collaboration diagram for Marmot::Discretizations::Meshfree::GradientEnhancedFiniteStrainParticleSQCNI:

Stabilized quasi-conforming nodal integration (SQCNI / SNNI) particle for gradient-enhanced finite-strain materials without a micropolar continuum.

The particle is a quadrilateral (2D) or hexahedral (3D) smoothing domain with one material point at its centroid. The weak forms, the material point and the tangent are those of GradientEnhancedFiniteStrainParticle; what changes is how the shape function gradients are obtained:

  • Smoothed gradients. As in stabilized conforming nodal integration (Chen et al., 2001), the gradient is replaced by its average over the smoothing domain \( \Omega_Y \), turned into a boundary integral by the divergence theorem and evaluated with one point per face \( f \) (the face center \( \boldsymbol{Y}_f \)),

    \[ \frac{\partial N_B}{\partial Y_i} \approx \frac{1}{V_{\Omega_Y}} \sum_f N_B(\boldsymbol{Y}_f)\,n_i\,dA_f . \]
    The shape function values \( N_B \) remain the point values at the center. Both the displacement gradient \( \Delta\boldsymbol{F} \) and the gradient of the nonlocal field use the smoothed gradients.

  • Faces. The particle has vertices and faces and therefore supports the distributed loads PRESSURE and CWFCORRECTION (computeDistributedLoad()); the point particle has none.

The smoothing domain is stored by the displacements of its vertices (appended to the state variables as vertex displacements) and is updated at each accepted increment from the deformation gradient \( \boldsymbol{F}_n \) of the material point, according to SmoothingDomainUpdateType: DeformationGradient (SQCNI: the domain follows \( \boldsymbol{F}_n \)), None (SNNI: the domain is only translated with the particle), RotationOnly (the rotation \( \boldsymbol{R} \) of the polar decomposition) and RotationAndPrincipalStretch ( \( \boldsymbol{R} \) times the diagonal of \( \boldsymbol{R}^\mathsf{T}\boldsymbol{F}_n \)). Only for DeformationGradient does the smoothing domain coincide with the physical domain of the particle.

class GradientEnhancedFiniteStrainParticleSQCNIxNSNI : public Marmot::Discretizations::Meshfree::GradientEnhancedFiniteStrainParticleSQCNI<nDim, nVertices>

Inheritance diagram for Marmot::Discretizations::Meshfree::GradientEnhancedFiniteStrainParticleSQCNIxNSNI:

Collaboration diagram for Marmot::Discretizations::Meshfree::GradientEnhancedFiniteStrainParticleSQCNIxNSNI:

SQCNI / SNNI particle with naturally stabilized nodal integration (NSNI) for gradient-enhanced finite-strain materials.

On top of the smoothed gradients of GradientEnhancedFiniteStrainParticleSQCNI, the momentum residual receives the NSNI stabilization term: the integrand is expanded to first order about the particle center, and the second-order term of the integral is kept, with the second moments of the particle domain. The second derivatives of the shape functions are smoothed like the first ones, by the gradients at the face centers,

\[ \frac{\partial^2 N_B}{\partial Y_I\,\partial Y_J} \approx \frac{1}{2V_{\Omega_Y}} \sum_f \Bigl( \frac{\partial N_B}{\partial Y_I}(\boldsymbol{Y}_f)\,n_J + \frac{\partial N_B}{\partial Y_J}(\boldsymbol{Y}_f)\,n_I \Bigr) dA_f \]
(symmetrized), and the Kirchhoff stress gradient along \( \boldsymbol{Y} \) is linearized as
\[ \frac{\partial\tau_{ij}}{\partial Y_K} \approx \frac{\partial\tau_{ij}}{\partial\Delta F_{mM}}\, \frac{\partial^2\Delta x_m}{\partial Y_M\,\partial Y_K}, \qquad \frac{\partial^2\Delta x_m}{\partial Y_M\,\partial Y_K} = \Delta q^U_{Bm}\, \frac{\partial^2 N_B}{\partial Y_M\,\partial Y_K} . \]
The stabilization residual added to \( r^U_{Aj} \) is
\[ r^{U,\mathrm{stab}}_{Aj} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}\Bigl(\frac{\partial N_A}{\partial Y_L}\Bigr) \frac{M^Y_{LK}}{J_Y}\,\frac{\partial\tau_{ij}}{\partial Y_K}, \qquad \frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}\Bigl(\frac{\partial N_A}{\partial Y_L}\Bigr) = \Delta F^{-1}_{mi}\,\frac{\partial^2 N_A}{\partial Y_m\,\partial Y_L}, \]
with the second moments \( \boldsymbol{M}^Y = J_Y\,\boldsymbol{F}_n\,\boldsymbol{M}^0\,\boldsymbol{F}_n^\mathsf{T} \) of the particle domain about its centroid in the intermediate reference configuration (updated at each accepted increment from those, \( \boldsymbol{M}^0 \), of the undeformed cell) and \( J_Y = \det\boldsymbol{F}_n \). Note that the stabilization uses the trial second derivatives for both the test and the trial side, i.e. it is not affected by VCI.

The stabilization acts on the momentum balance only; the nonlocal residual and its rows of the tangent are those of the SQCNI particle. The stress gradient does not contain the contribution \( \partial\boldsymbol{\tau}/\partial\bar{N}\,\nabla_Y\Delta\bar{N} \) of the nonlocal field.

The tangent of the stabilization term is APPROXIMATE by construction: it differentiates \( \partial^2\Delta\boldsymbol{x}/\partial\boldsymbol{Y}^2 \) and \( \Delta\boldsymbol{F}^{-1} \), but not \( \partial\boldsymbol{\tau}/\partial\Delta\boldsymbol{F} \) itself, i.e. it omits \( \partial^2\boldsymbol{\tau}/\partial\boldsymbol{F}^2 \) (not exposed by the material interface) and the dependence of \( \partial\boldsymbol{\tau}/\partial\Delta\boldsymbol{F} \) on \( \bar{N} \); the U-N block therefore has no stabilization contribution at all. The nonlocal rows are exact; the module test bounds the error of the U-U block and does not check the U-N block for this particle.

  • nDim – Spatial dimension (2: plane strain, 3: 3D).

  • nVertices – Number of vertices of the smoothing domain (4: Quad, 8: Hexa).
