Microplane model M7

Theory

The model implements the microplane model M7 for plain concrete of Caner and Bažant (2013), Microplane model M7 for plain concrete. I: Formulation and II: Calibration and verification, Journal of Engineering Mechanics, 139(12). The macroscopic strain increment is projected kinematically onto a set of microplanes (normal component \(\varepsilon_N\) and the two tangential components \(\varepsilon_M\), \(\varepsilon_L\)), on which an incremental elastic predictor is limited by the tensile, deviatoric, volumetric and frictional (shear) stress-strain boundaries of M7. The macroscopic stress follows from the principle of virtual work by homogenization over the microplanes.

The microplane discretization (normal vectors, weights and the normal-tangential projection tensors) is taken from the shared Marmot::ContinuumMechanics::Microplane module of MarmotMechanicsCore; the quadrature scheme is fixed at compile time to the 37-microplane scheme N37.

In addition to the original formulation, a closed-form transition function for the elastic unloading stiffness on the microplanes is used, which improves the convergence behavior in implicit simulations (Basche et al., 2026). The consistent tangent is computed analytically.

The constitutive laws are implemented in MicroplaneM7Formulation, which the gradient-enhanced extension GM7 reuses.

The fixed parameters \(c_1, \ldots, c_{21}\) (except \(c_3\)) take the values given by Caner and Bažant (2013).

Properties

The fixed parameters of Caner and Bažant (2013) are calibrated in N and mm. Therefore \(E\), \(f_c\) and transitionWidth have to be given in N/mm² (MPa); the M7 parameters \(\nu\), \(k_1, \ldots, k_5\), \(c_3\) and transitionExponent are dimensionless.

The material is registered as MICROPLANEM7 in the hypoelastic material factory. The state variables are the volumetric stress sig_v0, zeta, the microplane stresses sig_N0, sig_M0, sig_L0, the extremal normal strains eps_N0pos, eps_N0neg and the macroscopic strain strain_macro0. For postprocessing, activeTensileBoundary, activeCompressiveBoundary and activeShearBoundary hold the fractions of the microplanes on the tensile, compressive and shear boundary in the last increment.

class MicroplaneM7Formulation

Collaboration diagram for Marmot::Materials::MicroplaneM7Formulation:

The constitutive laws of the microplane model M7, shared by MicroplaneM7 and its gradient-enhanced extension GM7, which both hold an instance.

The base formulation follows Caner & Bažant (2013), “Microplane model M7 for

plain concrete. I: Formulation” and “II: Calibration and verification”, Journal of Engineering Mechanics, 139(12). The closed-form transition function of the unloading stiffness (see

transitionFunctionClosedForm) is introduced in Basche et al. (2026).

All functions are pure: they neither read nor write state variables. The normal laws and boundaries return the stress and its derivative w.r.t. the macroscopic strain they are evaluated with. The shear law splits its tangents by the strain they are derivatives with respect to: suffix _elastic for the strain of the elastic predictions (normal and shear), suffix _boundary for the strain the boundaries are evaluated with. For MicroplaneM7 both are the same strain, and the full tangent is the sum of the two parts, as all expressions are linear in the tangents passed in. GM7 evaluates the elastic predictions with the local and the boundaries with the over-nonlocal strain, so the two parts are its tangents w.r.t. the local and the over-nonlocal strain.
