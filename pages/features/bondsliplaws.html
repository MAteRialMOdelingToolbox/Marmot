Bond-Slip Laws

Bond-slip laws relate the slip of a reinforcement bar relative to its host to the bond stress on the bar surface, in the local frame of the bar (tangential, normal 1, normal 2). They derive from Marmot::Materials::BondSlipLaw, live as modules in the materials category and register in the Marmot::Registry::BondSlipLawFactory, from which the embedded bond element creates them by name.

LINEARELASTICBONDSLIP

Properties [K_t, K_n]: \(\tau_t = K_t s_t\), \(\sigma_n = K_n s_n\). Large stiffnesses give a penalty formulation of perfect bond.

MODELCODE2010BONDSLIP

Properties [tauMax, s1, s2, s3, alpha, tauF, K0, Kn, (mu, (pMax))] (8 to 10).

From fib Model Code 2010 (Eqs. 6.1-1 to 6.1-4) is only the monotonic envelope of the tangential bond stress over the slip:

\[\begin{split}\tau_{\mathrm{env}}(s) = \begin{cases} K_0\, s & 0 \le s \le s_0 \\ \tau_{\max} (s / s_1)^{\alpha} & s_0 < s \le s_1 \\ \tau_{\max} & s_1 < s \le s_2 \\ \tau_{\max} - (\tau_{\max} - \tau_f) \frac{s - s_2}{s_3 - s_2} & s_2 < s \le s_3 \\ \tau_f & s_3 < s \end{cases}\end{split}\]

Modelling choices, not from MC2010:

  • The linear branch \(K_0 s\) up to \(s_0\) (where \(K_0 s_0 = \tau_{\max}(s_0/s_1)^\alpha\)) regularizes the infinite initial slope of the power law. For \(\alpha = 1\) the power law is linear and \(s_0 = 0\).

  • Unloading and reloading are elastic with \(K_0\), inside the yield condition \(|\tau_t| \le \tau_{\mathrm{env}}(\kappa) + \mu p\) with \(\kappa = \max |s|\), the largest total slip reached in either direction. Beyond it, the slip is plastic (state plastic slip). Because \(\kappa\) is driven by the total slip, the yield stress of a step is known before the check and the return mapping is closed form. The damage is symmetric: after slip in one direction, the reversed direction is bounded by the same, already reduced bond stress. Cyclic degradation beyond that is not modelled.

  • The normal response is elastic with the penalty stiffness \(K_n\), \(\mathbf{t}_n = K_n (\mathbf{s}_n - \mathbf{s}_n^p)\), optionally perfectly plastic with an isotropic cap \(|\mathbf{t}_n| \le p_{\max}\) (radial return). The cap models local crushing of the concrete around the bar; \(\mathbf{s}_n^p\) (state plastic normal slip) is the permanent widening of the hole the bar sits in.

  • Coulomb friction: the contact pressure \(p = |\mathbf{t}_n|\) raises the bound by \(\mu p\), with the consistent coupling tangent \(\partial \tau_t / \partial \mathbf{s}_n\). The magnitude is used because a bar pushed sideways presses on the concrete on one side of its hole, whatever the sign of the normal slip. MC2010 itself accounts for transverse pressure by a factor \(\Omega_p\) on the envelope, which is not implemented.

With 8 properties, \(\mu = 0\) and there is no cap (linear normal response, \(K_n \ge 0\)). A cap \(p_{\max} > 0\) requires \(K_n > 0\).

For splitting, \(s_1 = s_2 = s(\tau_{\max})\) is the slip at the peak, taken from the pull-out branch. The law requires \(s_2 < s_3\), so \(s_3\) must exceed \(s_2\).

\(K_0\) and \(K_n\) are not MC2010 quantities but regularization and penalty choices, in N/mm³ for N, mm units. \(K_0\) must exceed the secant to the peak, \(K_0 > \tau_{\max}/s_1\); a few times that is usual (\(K_0\) sets the stiffness at small slip and of unloading). \(K_n\) should be large compared to the host stiffness per bar diameter, without ruining the conditioning. Example, \(f_{ck} = 30\) MPa, good bond, pull-out failure, units N and mm:

13.69, 1.0, 2.0, 10.0, 0.4, 5.48, 200.0, 1000.0

Sign convention

The slip is the displacement of the bar minus that of the host, in the local frame \([\mathbf{t}, \mathbf{n}_1, \mathbf{n}_2]\): the tangent \(\mathbf{t}\) points from the first to the second end node of the bar element (\(\mathbf{t} = \mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}/\mathrm{d}\eta\)), in 2D \(\mathbf{n}_1\) is \(\mathbf{t}\) rotated by +90°, in 3D the normals are an arbitrary right-handed pair (built from the global axis least aligned with \(\mathbf{t}\)); laws must therefore be isotropic in the normal plane. A positive bond stress acts on the host in the slip direction and on the bar against it.

Writing a new bond-slip law

No change to the bond elements or to EdelweissFE is needed: the elements create the law by name from the factory. Copy LinearElasticBondSlip as the template; a law is six files plus a section on this page.

  1. Create a module modules/materials/<Name> with a module.cmake (REQUIRES MarmotMechanicsCore), the header include/Marmot/<Name>.h, the source src/<Name>.cpp, the registration src/<Name>Registration.cpp, and the test test/test.cpp with its test.cmake.

  2. Derive from Marmot::Materials::BondSlipLaw. In the constructor, check the number of properties before reading them, validate them (throw std::invalid_argument) and declare the state variables in stateLayout, then stateLayout.finalize().

  3. Implement computeBondStress: state holds the values of the beginning of the increment on entry; update bondStress, the state variables, elasticEnergyDensity and dissipation, and return the algorithmic tangent dBondStress_dSlip. The slip increment and the time increment are passed for rate-dependent laws; the laws here are rate independent and ignore them. State and TimeIncrement are small structs of this interface (instead of state3D/timeInfo of the continuum materials) because a bond law has its own stress measure (a 3-vector traction per bar surface).

  4. Register it in a registration source (see ModelCode2010BondSlipRegistration.cpp) with BondSlipLawFactory::registerBondSlipLaw<Name>("NAME").

Verification

Each law has a test in its module, run with ctest: the closed-form envelope on a monotonic path, unloading, reversal, the Coulomb envelope and the normal cap, the rejection of invalid properties, and the tangent compared to central differences on every branch, starting from committed states. A new law should at least check its tangent this way.

In a structure, a linear law can be checked against the analytical pull-out of an elastic bar (axial stiffness \(EA\), bonded length \(L\), perimeter \(p\)) from a rigid host: the end force over the end displacement is

\[\frac{F}{u} = EA\, \beta \tanh(\beta L), \qquad \beta = \sqrt{\frac{K_t\, p}{EA}},\]

to which the embedded bond element converges as the bar elements are refined (EdelweissFE test tests/test_embedded_bond.py).

Implementation

class BondSlipLaw

Abstract base class for bond-slip laws, i.e., the constitutive relation between the slip of a reinforcement bar relative to its host continuum and the bond stress acting on the bar surface.

A bond-slip law operates in the local frame of the bar: the first component is the tangential slip along the bar axis, the second and third components are the relative displacements normal to the bar axis. In two dimensions the third component is zero. The returned bond stress is a traction per unit bar surface; the element integrates it over the bar perimeter and length.

Bond-slip laws are pluggable: they live as modules in the materials category and register themselves in the Marmot::Registry::BondSlipLawFactory, from which bond elements create them by name.

The interface has its own small State and TimeIncrement structs instead of the state3D/timeInfo of the continuum materials, because its stress measure is a traction vector per unit bar surface, not a stress tensor.

class LinearElasticBondSlip : public Marmot::Materials::BondSlipLaw

Linear elastic bond-slip law.

\[ \tau_t = K_t\, s_t, \qquad \sigma_{n,i} = K_n\, s_{n,i} \]

With large stiffnesses it is a penalty formulation of perfect bond.

Material properties:

  1. K_t: tangential bond stiffness (stress per slip)

  2. K_n: normal bond stiffness (stress per relative displacement)

No state variables.

class ModelCode2010BondSlip : public Marmot::Materials::BondSlipLaw

Bond-slip law with the monotonic envelope of the fib Model Code 2010 (Sec. 6.1.1), elastic unloading and optional pressure-dependent (Coulomb) friction with a limited normal stress.

From fib MC2010 (Eqs. 6.1-1 to 6.1-4) is only the monotonic envelope of the tangential bond stress \(\tau_t\) over the slip \(s \geq 0\):

\[\begin{split} \tau_{\mathrm{env}}(s) = \begin{cases} K_0\, s & 0 \leq s \leq s_0 \\ \tau_{\max} \left( s / s_1 \right)^{\alpha} & s_0 < s \leq s_1 \\ \tau_{\max} & s_1 < s \leq s_2 \\ \tau_{\max} - (\tau_{\max} - \tau_f) \frac{s - s_2}{s_3 - s_2} & s_2 < s \leq s_3 \\ \tau_f & s_3 < s \end{cases} \end{split}\]
Parameters for the envelope are given in MC2010 Table 6.1-1, see the documentation page on bond-slip laws.

Modelling choices, not from MC2010:

  • The linear branch with the stiffness \(K_0\) regularizes the infinite initial slope of the power law; \(s_0\) is where both meet ( \(K_0 s_0 = \tau_{\max} (s_0/s_1)^\alpha\); \(s_0 = 0\) for \(\alpha = 1\)).

  • Unloading and reloading are elastic with \(K_0\), \(\tau_t = K_0 (s - s_p)\), inside the yield condition \(|\tau_t| \leq \tau_{\mathrm{env}}(\kappa) + \mu p\), where \(\kappa = \max |s|\) is the largest total slip ever reached in either direction. The bond damage is therefore symmetric: the reversed direction is bounded by the same, already reduced bond stress. Cyclic degradation beyond that is not modelled.

  • The normal behavior is elastic with the penalty stiffness \(K_n\), \(\boldsymbol{t}_n = K_n (\boldsymbol{s}_n - \boldsymbol{s}_n^p)\), optionally perfectly plastic with an isotropic cap \(|\boldsymbol{t}_n| \leq p_{\max}\) in the normal plane (radial return). The cap models local crushing of the concrete around the bar: the plastic normal slip \(\boldsymbol{s}_n^p\) is the permanent widening of the hole the bar sits in.

  • Coulomb friction \(\mu p\) with the contact pressure \(p = |\boldsymbol{t}_n|\) raises the bond bound. The magnitude is used because a bar pushed sideways presses against the concrete on one side of its hole, whatever the sign of the normal slip. MC2010 accounts for transverse pressure by its own factor \(\Omega_p\) instead, which is not implemented.

With 8 properties, \(\mu = 0\) and the normal behavior is linear elastic.

Material properties:

  1. tauMax: bond strength \(\tau_{\max}\)

  2. s1, 2. s2, 3. s3: characteristic slips, \(0 < s_1 \leq s_2 < s_3\)

  3. alpha: exponent of the ascending branch, \(0 \leq \alpha \leq 1\)

  4. tauF: residual bond stress, \(0 \leq \tau_f \leq \tau_{\max}\)

  5. K0: initial and unloading bond stiffness (penalty), \(K_0 > \tau_{\max} / s_1\)

  6. Kn: normal stiffness (penalty), \(K_n \geq 0\)

  7. mu (optional, default 0): friction coefficient, \(\mu \geq 0\)

  8. pMax (optional, default 0): largest normal stress, \(\leq 0\) for none; \(p_{\max} > 0\) requires \(K_n > 0\)

State variables: plastic slip \(s_p\), max slip \(\kappa\), plastic normal slip \(\boldsymbol{s}_n^p\) (2).
