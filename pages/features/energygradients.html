Energy gradients

Gradients of the elastic energy and the dissipation of an element with respect to its degrees of freedom, consistent with the algorithmic update. They linearize constraints on the energies of a structure, e.g., the dissipation based arc-length control of EdelweissFE, where an increment dissipates a prescribed amount of energy.

They are optional capabilities: elements and materials that provide them inherit from an additional interface class. The standard interfaces (Element, MaterialHypoElastic, MaterialGradientEnhancedHypoElastic) are unchanged, and elements and materials without the capability behave exactly as before.

Theory

The element’s elastic energy and dissipation are the sums over its quadrature points,

\[\Psi = \sum_{q} \psi_q\, J_0 w_q, \qquad D = \sum_{q} D_q\, J_0 w_q,\]

with the energy densities \(\psi_q\) and \(D_q\) after the update of the increment. Their gradients follow with the kinematics of the element, e.g., \(\Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} = \boldsymbol{B}\,\Delta\boldsymbol{q}\) for the displacement element,

\[\frac{\partial \Psi}{\partial \boldsymbol{q}} = \sum_{q} \boldsymbol{B}^T \frac{\partial \psi_q}{\partial \Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}\, J_0 w_q, \qquad \frac{\partial D}{\partial \boldsymbol{q}} = \sum_{q} \boldsymbol{B}^T \frac{\partial D_q}{\partial \Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}\, J_0 w_q.\]

The gradient-enhanced element adds the derivatives with respect to the non-local variables, interpolated with the shape functions of the non-local field. The material provides the derivatives of its energy densities, the energy tangents, consistent with its stress update; they depend on the history, so they are not the derivatives of a potential.

Plane stress

In plane stress, the material’s update is iterated until \(\sigma_{33} = 0\), which determines the out-of-plane strain increment \(\Delta\varepsilon_{33}\) as a function of the in-plane strain increment and the non-local variables. The energy tangents of the converged 3D update are condensed by the total derivative

\[\frac{\mathrm{d} g}{\mathrm{d} \Delta\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}} = \frac{\partial g}{\partial \Delta\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}} + \frac{\partial g}{\partial \Delta\varepsilon_{33}} \frac{\partial \Delta\varepsilon_{33}}{\partial \Delta\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}}, \qquad \frac{\mathrm{d} g}{\mathrm{d} \bar\kappa} = \frac{\partial g}{\partial \bar\kappa} + \frac{\partial g}{\partial \Delta\varepsilon_{33}} \frac{\partial \Delta\varepsilon_{33}}{\partial \bar\kappa},\]

for \(g = \psi, D\), with \(\partial \Delta\varepsilon_{33} / \partial (\cdot) = -(\partial \sigma_{33} / \partial (\cdot)) / (\partial \sigma_{33} / \partial \Delta\varepsilon_{33})\) from \(\mathrm{d}\sigma_{33} = 0\).

Usage

A framework finds the capability of an element once, assigns two arrays of the size of the element’s degrees of freedom, and reads them after each evaluation of the element:

using namespace Marmot::Discretizations::Elements;

// after the material section is assigned
auto* provider = dynamic_cast< EnergyGradientProvider* >( element );
if ( !provider )
  ; // the element provides no energy gradients

std::vector< double > dElasticEnergy_dQ( element->getNDofPerElement() );
std::vector< double > dDissipation_dQ( element->getNDofPerElement() );
provider->assignEnergyGradientOutput( dElasticEnergy_dQ.data(), dDissipation_dQ.data() );

element->computeKernels( QTotal, dQ, Pe, Ke, time, dT ); // also writes both gradients

provider->assignEnergyGradientOutput( nullptr, nullptr ); // stops the output

  • When they are written: in every call of computeKernels(), for the state of that call, i.e., of the current iterate. The arrays are overwritten, not accumulated. computeKernelsExplicit() does not write them. Elements without assigned outputs evaluate their kernels as before.

  • Order: the element’s own order of the degrees of freedom, as the residual Pe of computeKernels(); the framework maps them to its global vector as the residual (Element::getDofIndicesPermutationPattern()).

  • Checks: assignEnergyGradientOutput() throws std::invalid_argument if the section or the material cannot provide the gradients, so an unsupported setup is rejected before the analysis. A material section assigned while the outputs are active is checked as well; if it is rejected, the element keeps its material.

  • Lifetime: the element writes to the arrays until the outputs are reset with nullptr, so they must outlive the assignment.

GradientEnhancedHypoElasticEnergyTangentProvider

Materials with energy tangents: Von Mises model, Rankine softening plasticity and Rankine damage-plasticity (HypoElasticEnergyTangentProvider), and their gradient-enhanced versions (GradientEnhancedHypoElasticEnergyTangentProvider).

A material provides the energy tangents by inheriting from the capability class next to its base class and implementing computeStressWithEnergyTangents(), a variant of computeStress() that also returns the derivatives of the updated energy densities. Both should share one implementation of the update, so that they give the same stress. For plane stress, GradientEnhancedHypoElasticEnergyTangentProvider::computePlaneStressWithEnergyTangents() runs the plane stress iteration of MaterialGradientEnhancedHypoElastic::computePlaneStress() around the variant; a material that overrides computePlaneStress() overrides it consistently.

Implementation

class EnergyGradientProvider

Optional capability of an Element: the gradients of its elastic energy and dissipation wrt its dofs.

An element provides the gradients by inheriting from this class next to Element. Frameworks find the capability with dynamic_cast< EnergyGradientProvider* >( element ), e.g., for dissipation based arc length control, and assign the output arrays once. The element then writes the gradients in every computeKernels() call, for the state of that call, consistent with its algorithmic update; computeKernelsExplicit() does not write them. Elements without assigned outputs evaluate their kernels as before, and the standard element interface is not affected.

Subclassed by Marmot::Discretizations::Elements::DisplacementFiniteElement< nDim, nNodes >, Marmot::Discretizations::Elements::GeneralGradientEnhancedDisplacementFiniteElement< nDim, nNodes, nNonlocalVariables, nNonLocalNodes >

Public Functions

virtual ~EnergyGradientProvider() = default
virtual void assignEnergyGradientOutput(double *dElasticEnergy_dQ, double *dDissipation_dQ) = 0

Assign the arrays the element writes its energy gradients to in every computeKernels() call.

Call it after the material section is assigned. It throws std::invalid_argument if the section or the material cannot provide the gradients, so unsupported setups are rejected before the analysis. The arrays must outlive the assignment.

Parameters:

  • dElasticEnergy_dQ – Gradient of the elastic energy, of size Element::getNDofPerElement(), in the element’s dof order; nullptr, together with dDissipation_dQ, stops the output.

  • dDissipation_dQ – Gradient of the dissipation, of the same size and order.

class HypoElasticEnergyTangentProvider

Optional capability of a MaterialHypoElastic: a stress update that also yields the energy tangents.

A material provides the derivatives of its updated elastic energy density and dissipation wrt the strain increment by inheriting from this class next to MaterialHypoElastic. Elements find the capability with dynamic_cast< const HypoElasticEnergyTangentProvider* >( material ) and call this variant instead of MaterialHypoElastic::computeStress only when they assemble energy gradients, e.g., for dissipation based arc length control. The standard material interface is not affected.

Subclassed by Marmot::Materials::RankineDamagePlasticity, Marmot::Materials::RankineSofteningPlasticity, Marmot::Materials::VonMisesModel

class GradientEnhancedHypoElasticEnergyTangentProvider

Optional capability of a MaterialGradientEnhancedHypoElastic: a stress update that also yields the energy tangents.

A material provides the derivatives of its updated elastic energy density and dissipation wrt the strain increment and the nonlocal variables by inheriting from this class next to MaterialGradientEnhancedHypoElastic. Elements find the capability with dynamic_cast and call this variant instead of MaterialGradientEnhancedHypoElastic::computeStress only when they assemble energy gradients. The standard material interface is not affected.

Template Parameters:

MaterialGradientEnhancedHypoElastic::computePlaneStress, which also yields the energy tangents of the converged 3D update.

The default is the plane stress iteration of MaterialGradientEnhancedHypoElastic::computePlaneStress (MaterialGradientEnhancedHypoElastic::iteratePlaneStress) around computeStressWithEnergyTangents(). A material that overrides MaterialGradientEnhancedHypoElastic::computePlaneStress overrides this method consistently, so that the stress does not depend on whether energy tangents are requested. The class providing the capability must also be a MaterialGradientEnhancedHypoElastic.

Parameters:

  • res – [inout] Response, as for MaterialGradientEnhancedHypoElastic::computePlaneStress.

  • tan – [inout] Algorithmic tangents of the converged 3D update.

  • inc – [in] Increment of the in-plane strain and the nonlocal variables.

  • energyTan – [out] Derivatives of the updated elastic energy density and dissipation wrt the 3D strain increment and the nonlocal variables; the condensation of the out-of-plane strain is left to the element.
