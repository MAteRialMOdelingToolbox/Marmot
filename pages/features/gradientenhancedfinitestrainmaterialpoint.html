Gradient-Enhanced Finite-Strain Material Point

Theory

The gradient-enhanced finite-strain material point carries the displacement \(\boldsymbol u\) and \(n_\mathrm{nonlocal}\) nonlocal fields \(\bar N_K\) and drives a material of the interface Marmot::Materials::MaterialGradientEnhancedFiniteStrain<nNonlocal>: with one nonlocal variable e.g. Gradient-enhanced compressible Neo-Hooke damage or Gradient-enhanced finite-strain Drucker-Prager, with six (a nonlocal strain tensor) GM7/COROTATIONAL (Gradient-enhanced microplane model M7 (GM7), Gradient-enhanced corotational wrapper). The material returns the Kirchhoff stress \(\boldsymbol\tau\), the local driving forces \(L_K\), the nonlocal radii \(R_K\) (with the interactions \(c_K = R_K^2\)) and the four algorithmic tangents of the implicit-gradient problem

\[\bar N_K - c_K\,\nabla_X^2 \bar N_K = L_K(\boldsymbol F, \bar{\boldsymbol N}).\]

Quantities of the nonlocal space are Eigen vectors of size \(n_\mathrm{nonlocal}\); a tangent that mixes a spatial tensor with a nonlocal variable is one tensor per nonlocal variable (tangents.dS_dN[K], tangents.dL_dDeltaF[K]).

It is the material point of the MPM cell Gradient-Enhanced Finite-Strain Cell and is owned by each RKPM particle of Gradient-Enhanced Finite-Strain Particles. The weak forms themselves are assembled there; the material point provides the kinematics and the constitutive response. See MPM Cells and Material Points for the material point interface and Gradient-Enhanced Finite-Strain Displacement Element for the finite-element counterpart of the formulation.

Kinematics

The material point follows the semi-Lagrangian convention of the meshfree layer. The state stores the deformation gradient \(\boldsymbol F_n = \partial\boldsymbol Y/\partial\boldsymbol X\) of the last accepted state (the intermediate reference configuration \(\boldsymbol Y\)) and the increment \(\Delta\boldsymbol F = \partial\boldsymbol x/\partial\boldsymbol Y\) of the current step. The host (a cell or a particle) interpolates the increments of the current step and adds them with incrementDeformation,

\[\Delta F_{ij} = \delta_{ij} + \frac{\partial \Delta u_i}{\partial Y_j}, \qquad \bar N_K = \bar N_{K,n} + \Delta\bar N_K, \qquad \nabla_X \bar N_K \mathrel{+}= \boldsymbol F_n^T \nabla_Y \Delta\bar N_K,\]

and the material is evaluated at

\[\boldsymbol F = \Delta\boldsymbol F\,\boldsymbol F_n .\]

prepareYourself resets \(\Delta\boldsymbol u = \boldsymbol 0\) and \(\Delta\boldsymbol F = \boldsymbol I\) (the nonlocal fields and their gradients are stored as totals and are not reset); acceptStateAndPosition sets \(\boldsymbol u \leftarrow \boldsymbol u + \Delta\boldsymbol u\) and \(\boldsymbol F_n \leftarrow \Delta\boldsymbol F\,\boldsymbol F_n\).

Response and tangents

After computeYourself the material point exposes

  • response.S: the Kirchhoff stress \(\boldsymbol\tau\),

  • response.dL: \(\Delta L_K\), the driving forces of this evaluation minus the values stored in the state variable local damage (local strain), which is then overwritten by the new values. It is the increment of \(L_K\) over the step as long as the host evaluates the point from the state of the last accepted increment,

  • response.nonLocalRadius: \(R_K\),

and the tangents with respect to the unknowns of the increment. Since \(\boldsymbol F = \Delta\boldsymbol F\, \boldsymbol F_n\), the material tangents with respect to \(\boldsymbol F\) are converted by the chain rule

\[\frac{\partial F_{iI}}{\partial \Delta F_{jJ}} = \delta_{ij}\,F_{n,JI}, \qquad \frac{\partial\tau_{ij}}{\partial\Delta F_{kL}} = \frac{\partial\tau_{ij}}{\partial F_{mn}} \frac{\partial F_{mn}}{\partial\Delta F_{kL}}, \qquad \frac{\partial L}{\partial\Delta F_{kL}} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial F_{mn}} \frac{\partial F_{mn}}{\partial\Delta F_{kL}},\]

while \(\partial\boldsymbol\tau/\partial\bar N\) and \(\partial L/\partial\bar N\) are passed on unchanged (fields tangents.dS_dDeltaF, tangents.dS_dN, tangents.dL_dDeltaF, tangents.dL_dN).

In plane strain all quantities are stored in 3D, the material is evaluated through computePlaneStrain (the 3D response of a deformation gradient whose out-of-plane entries are those of the identity), and the in-plane components of the response and the tangents are handed out.

Initialization

initializeYourself sets \(\boldsymbol F_n = \boldsymbol I\), a unit eigen deformation and a zero increment, initializes the material state and queries the reference density from the material, so that the inertia can be assembled before the first computation. The density is updated after each computeYourself. The undeformed volume \(V_0\) and coordinates \(\boldsymbol X\) are set at construction.

The total gradient \(\nabla_X\bar N_K\) and the rate \(\dot{\bar N}_K\) are kept for the explicit scheme of the particles, whose nonlocal residual is written in total form: the gradient is accumulated by incrementDeformation in every scheme, so that an explicit step may follow an implicit one.

The initial condition geostaticstress finds, through the material, the eigen deformation that produces a hydrostatic stress \(\tau_{XX} = \tau_{YY} = \tau_{ZZ} =\) value[0] and applies it in all subsequent material evaluations.

Implementation

template<int nDim, int nNonlocal>
class GradientEnhancedFiniteStrainMaterialPoint : public Marmot::Discretizations::MaterialPoints::MaterialPoint

Inheritance diagram for Marmot::Discretizations::MaterialPoints::GradientEnhancedFiniteStrainMaterialPoint:

Collaboration diagram for Marmot::Discretizations::MaterialPoints::GradientEnhancedFiniteStrainMaterialPoint:

Material point for gradient-enhanced (implicit-gradient) finite-strain materials without a micropolar continuum.

The material point carries the displacement field \( \boldsymbol{u} \) and nNonlocal nonlocal fields \( \bar{N}_K \) and drives a Marmot::Materials::MaterialGradientEnhancedFiniteStrain, which returns the Kirchhoff stress \( \boldsymbol{\tau} \), the local driving forces \( L_K \) (the sources of \( \bar{N}_K - c_K\,\nabla^2 \bar{N}_K = L_K \), \( c_K = R_K^2 \) with the nonlocal radius \( R_K \)) and the four algorithmic tangents. It is the material point both of the MPM cell GradientEnhancedFiniteStrainCell and of the RKPM particle GradientEnhancedFiniteStrainParticle (and its SQCNI / NSNI variants). There is no micro-rotation field and hence no couple stress.

Kinematics (semi-Lagrangian). The state variable deformation gradient (dY_dX) holds the deformation gradient \( \boldsymbol{F}_n = \partial\boldsymbol{Y}/\partial\boldsymbol{X} \) of the last accepted state, i.e. of the intermediate reference configuration \( \boldsymbol{Y} \) with respect to the undeformed configuration \( \boldsymbol{X} \), and delta deformation gradient (dx_dY) the increment \( \Delta\boldsymbol{F} = \partial\boldsymbol{x}/\partial\boldsymbol{Y} \) accumulated since. The material is evaluated at

\[ \boldsymbol{F} = \Delta\boldsymbol{F}\,\boldsymbol{F}_n, \qquad \bar{N}_K = \bar{N}_{K,n} + \Delta\bar{N}_K, \]
where \( \Delta F_{ij} = \delta_{ij} + \partial\Delta u_i/\partial Y_j \) and \( \Delta\bar{N}_K \) are accumulated by incrementDeformation() from the increments interpolated by the cell or particle. Because the unknowns of an increment are \( \Delta\boldsymbol{F} \) and \( \Delta\bar{N}_K \), computeYourself() converts the stress and driving-force tangents with respect to \( \boldsymbol{F} \) to tangents with respect to \( \Delta\boldsymbol{F} \) by the chain rule
\[ \frac{\partial F_{iI}}{\partial \Delta F_{jJ}} = \delta_{ij}\,F_{n,JI}, \]
and stores them in tangents together with the response in response.

Totals for the explicit scheme. The material point also keeps the running sum of \( \nabla_X\Delta\bar{N}_K \) (state <nonlocal> gradient, i.e. \( \nabla_X\bar{N}_K \)) and the rate \( \dot{\bar{N}}_K \) of the last explicit increment (state <nonlocal> rate): the explicit residual of the nonlocal balance is written in total form, and the momentum of a nonlocal field with a micro-inertia needs its rate. The gradient is accumulated by incrementDeformation() in the implicit and the explicit scheme alike, so that an explicit step may follow an implicit one.

In plane strain (nDim = 2) all tensors are stored in 3D, the material is evaluated through Marmot::Materials::MaterialGradientEnhancedFiniteStrain::computePlaneStrain, and the in-plane parts are handed out.

Registered as GradientEnhancedFiniteStrain/PlaneStrain and GradientEnhancedFiniteStrain/3D (one nonlocal variable) and as GradientEnhanced6FiniteStrain/3D (six).

Template Parameters:

  • nDim – Spatial dimension (2: plane strain, 3: 3D).

  • nNonlocal – Number of nonlocal variables (1: a scalar nonlocal damage driver, 6: a nonlocal strain).

inline virtual void acceptStateAndPosition() override

Accept the current increment: \( \boldsymbol{u} \leftarrow \boldsymbol{u} + \Delta\boldsymbol{u} \), \( \boldsymbol{F}_n \leftarrow \Delta\boldsymbol{F}\,\boldsymbol{F}_n \).
