Gradient-Enhanced Finite-Strain Material Point

Theory

The gradient-enhanced finite-strain material point carries the displacement \(\boldsymbol u\) and a single scalar nonlocal field \(\bar N\) and drives a material of the interface Marmot::Materials::MaterialGradientEnhancedFiniteStrain, e.g. Gradient-enhanced compressible Neo-Hooke damage or Gradient-enhanced finite-strain Drucker-Prager. The material returns the Kirchhoff stress \(\boldsymbol\tau\), the local driving force \(L\), the nonlocal radius \(R\) (with the interaction \(c = R^2\)) and the four algorithmic tangents of the implicit-gradient problem

\[\bar N - c\,\nabla_X^2 \bar N = L(\boldsymbol F, \bar N).\]

It is the material point of the MPM cell Gradient-Enhanced Finite-Strain Cell and is owned by each RKPM particle of Gradient-Enhanced Finite-Strain Particles. The weak forms themselves are assembled there; the material point provides the kinematics and the constitutive response. See MPM Cells and Material Points for the material point interface and Gradient-Enhanced Finite-Strain Displacement Element for the finite-element counterpart of the formulation.

Kinematics

The material point follows the semi-Lagrangian convention of the meshfree layer. The state stores the deformation gradient \(\boldsymbol F_n = \partial\boldsymbol Y/\partial\boldsymbol X\) of the last accepted state (the intermediate reference configuration \(\boldsymbol Y\)) and the increment \(\Delta\boldsymbol F = \partial\boldsymbol x/\partial\boldsymbol Y\) of the current step. The host (a cell or a particle) interpolates the increments of the current step and adds them with incrementDeformation,

\[\Delta F_{ij} = \delta_{ij} + \frac{\partial \Delta u_i}{\partial Y_j}, \qquad \bar N = \bar N_n + \Delta\bar N,\]

and the material is evaluated at

\[\boldsymbol F = \Delta\boldsymbol F\,\boldsymbol F_n .\]

prepareYourself resets \(\Delta\boldsymbol u = \boldsymbol 0\) and \(\Delta\boldsymbol F = \boldsymbol I\) (the nonlocal field is stored as a total and is not reset); acceptStateAndPosition sets \(\boldsymbol u \leftarrow \boldsymbol u + \Delta\boldsymbol u\) and \(\boldsymbol F_n \leftarrow \Delta\boldsymbol F\,\boldsymbol F_n\).

Response and tangents

After computeYourself the material point exposes

  • response.S: the Kirchhoff stress \(\boldsymbol\tau\),

  • response.dL: \(\Delta L\), the driving force of this evaluation minus the value stored in the state variable local damage, which is then overwritten by the new value. It is the increment of \(L\) over the step as long as the host evaluates the point from the state of the last accepted increment,

  • response.nonLocalRadius: \(R\),

and the tangents with respect to the unknowns of the increment. Since \(\boldsymbol F = \Delta\boldsymbol F\, \boldsymbol F_n\), the material tangents with respect to \(\boldsymbol F\) are converted by the chain rule

\[\frac{\partial F_{iI}}{\partial \Delta F_{jJ}} = \delta_{ij}\,F_{n,JI}, \qquad \frac{\partial\tau_{ij}}{\partial\Delta F_{kL}} = \frac{\partial\tau_{ij}}{\partial F_{mn}} \frac{\partial F_{mn}}{\partial\Delta F_{kL}}, \qquad \frac{\partial L}{\partial\Delta F_{kL}} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial F_{mn}} \frac{\partial F_{mn}}{\partial\Delta F_{kL}},\]

while \(\partial\boldsymbol\tau/\partial\bar N\) and \(\partial L/\partial\bar N\) are passed on unchanged (fields tangents.dS_dDeltaF, tangents.dS_dN, tangents.dL_dDeltaF, tangents.dL_dN).

In plane strain all quantities are stored in 3D, the material is evaluated through computePlaneStrain (the 3D response of a deformation gradient whose out-of-plane entries are those of the identity), and the in-plane components of the response and the tangents are handed out.

Initialization

initializeYourself sets \(\boldsymbol F_n = \boldsymbol I\), a unit eigen deformation and a zero increment, initializes the material state and queries the reference density from the material, so that the inertia can be assembled before the first computation. The density is updated after each computeYourself. The undeformed volume \(V_0\) and coordinates \(\boldsymbol X\) are set at construction.

The initial condition geostaticstress finds, through the material, the eigen deformation that produces a hydrostatic stress \(\tau_{XX} = \tau_{YY} = \tau_{ZZ} =\) value[0] and applies it in all subsequent material evaluations.

Material point for gradient-enhanced (implicit-gradient) finite-strain materials without a micropolar continuum.

The material point carries the displacement field \( \boldsymbol{u} \) and a single scalar nonlocal field \( \bar{N} \) and drives a Marmot::Materials::MaterialGradientEnhancedFiniteStrain, which returns the Kirchhoff stress \( \boldsymbol{\tau} \), the local driving force \( L \) (the source of \( \bar{N} - c\,\nabla^2\bar{N} = L \), \( c = R^2 \) with the nonlocal radius \( R \)) and the four algorithmic tangents. It is the material point both of the MPM cell GradientEnhancedFiniteStrainCell and of the RKPM particle GradientEnhancedFiniteStrainParticle (and its SQCNI / NSNI variants). There is no micro-rotation field and hence no couple stress.

Kinematics (semi-Lagrangian). The state variable deformation gradient (dY_dX) holds the deformation gradient \( \boldsymbol{F}_n = \partial\boldsymbol{Y}/\partial\boldsymbol{X} \) of the last accepted state, i.e. of the intermediate reference configuration \( \boldsymbol{Y} \) with respect to the undeformed configuration \( \boldsymbol{X} \), and delta deformation gradient (dx_dY) the increment \( \Delta\boldsymbol{F} = \partial\boldsymbol{x}/\partial\boldsymbol{Y} \) accumulated since. The material is evaluated at

\[ \boldsymbol{F} = \Delta\boldsymbol{F}\,\boldsymbol{F}_n, \qquad \bar{N} = \bar{N}_n + \Delta\bar{N}, \]
where \( \Delta F_{ij} = \delta_{ij} + \partial\Delta u_i/\partial Y_j \) and \( \Delta\bar{N} \) are accumulated by incrementDeformation() from the increments interpolated by the cell or particle. Because the unknowns of an increment are \( \Delta\boldsymbol{F} \) and \( \Delta\bar{N} \), computeYourself() converts the stress and driving-force tangents with respect to \( \boldsymbol{F} \) to tangents with respect to \( \Delta\boldsymbol{F} \) by the chain rule
\[ \frac{\partial F_{iI}}{\partial \Delta F_{jJ}} = \delta_{ij}\,F_{n,JI}, \]
and stores them in tangents together with the response in response.

In plane strain (nDim = 2) all tensors are stored in 3D, the material is evaluated through Marmot::Materials::MaterialGradientEnhancedFiniteStrain::computePlaneStrain, and the in-plane parts are handed out.

Template Parameters:

nDim – Spatial dimension (2: plane strain, 3: 3D).
