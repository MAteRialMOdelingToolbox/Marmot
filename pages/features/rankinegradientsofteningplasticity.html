Gradient-enhanced Rankine softening plasticity model

Implicit-gradient, over-nonlocal version of the Rankine softening plasticity model: multi-surface Rankine plasticity whose yield stress depends on a combination of the local and the nonlocal softening variable (Vermeer and Brinkgreve, 1994), for the general gradient-enhanced displacement element.

Theory

The yield functions, the associated flow rule and the local softening variable \(\kappa\), with \(\Delta\kappa = \sum_A \Delta\lambda_A\), are those of the Rankine softening plasticity model. The local softening variable is the source of the Helmholtz equation solved by the element,

\[\bar\kappa - l^2\,\Delta\bar\kappa = \kappa, \qquad \nabla\bar\kappa\cdot\boldsymbol{n} = 0 \text{ on the boundary}.\]

The yield stress follows from the over-nonlocal combination

\[\hat\kappa = m\,\bar\kappa + (1-m)\,\kappa, \qquad f_\mathrm{y}\left(\hat\kappa\right) = \max\left(f_\mathrm{y}^0 + H_\mathrm{iso,lin}\hat\kappa + \Delta f_\mathrm{y}^{0,\infty}\left(1-\exp\left(-\delta\hat\kappa\right)\right),\ 0\right).\]

For \(m = 0\) the model is the Rankine softening plasticity model. For a softening law and \(m > 1\), the yield stress increases with \(\kappa\) at a fixed nonlocal variable: the material point hardens locally, and softening enters by the nonlocal variable only, which prevents the plastic strain from localizing in a set of measure zero. The yield stress is floored at zero in terms of \(\hat\kappa\), and the dissipated plastic work is \(\boldsymbol{\sigma}:\Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^\mathrm{p} = f_\mathrm{y}\left(\hat\kappa\right)\Delta\kappa\).

Implementation

Stress Update Algorithm

The backward Euler return mapping in principal space is that of the Rankine softening plasticity model, with the residuals

\[r_A = \sigma_A^\mathrm{trial} - \sum_{B\in\mathcal{A}} C^\mathrm{e}_{AB}\Delta\lambda_B - f_\mathrm{y}\left(m\,\bar\kappa + (1-m)\left(\kappa^n + \sum_B\Delta\lambda_B\right)\right) = 0\]

for all surfaces in the active set \(\mathcal{A}\), and the Jacobian

\[J_{AB} = C^\mathrm{e}_{AB} + (1-m)\,\frac{\partial f_\mathrm{y}}{\partial\hat\kappa}, \qquad A,B\in\mathcal{A}.\]

Since the yield stress depends on the nonlocal variable, an increment without a strain increment may be plastic.

Consistent Algorithmic Tangent Operator

The tangent \(\partial\boldsymbol{\sigma}/\partial\Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\) follows as for the Rankine softening plasticity model, with the hardening modulus \((1-m)\,\partial f_\mathrm{y}/\partial\hat\kappa\). The nonlocal variable shifts the yield stress of the active surfaces, which gives the coupling terms

\[\frac{\partial\Delta\lambda_A}{\partial\bar\kappa} = -m\,\frac{\partial f_\mathrm{y}}{\partial\hat\kappa} \sum_{B\in\mathcal{A}} J^{-1}_{AB}, \qquad \frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\sigma}}{\partial\bar\kappa} = -\sum_{A}\sum_{B\in\mathcal{A}} C^\mathrm{e}_{AB}\,\frac{\partial\Delta\lambda_B}{\partial\bar\kappa}\, \boldsymbol{n}_A\otimes\boldsymbol{n}_A, \qquad \frac{\partial\kappa}{\partial\bar\kappa} = \sum_{A\in\mathcal{A}} \frac{\partial\Delta\lambda_A}{\partial\bar\kappa}.\]

Since the flow directions have unit trace, \(\Delta\kappa = \mathrm{tr}\left(\Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} - \mathbb{C}^{-1}:\Delta\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right)\), and

\[\frac{\partial\kappa}{\partial\Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}} = \boldsymbol{1} - \left(\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\sigma}}{\partial\Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}\right)^T : \mathbb{C}^{-1} : \boldsymbol{1}.\]

The model is registered as RANKINEGRADIENTSOFTENINGPLASTICITY in the gradient-enhanced hypoelastic factory with one nonlocal field.

Over-nonlocal (implicit gradient) version of RankineSofteningPlasticity: multi-surface Rankine plasticity whose yield stress depends on a combination of the local and the nonlocal softening variable, following Vermeer and Brinkgreve (1994) and the structure of RankineGradientDamagePlasticity.

The three Rankine surfaces \( f_A = \sigma_A - f_\mathrm{y}(\hat\kappa) \) with associated flow and \( \Delta\kappa = \sum_A \Delta\lambda_A \) are those of RankineSofteningPlasticity. The local softening variable \( \kappa \) is the source of the Helmholtz equation solved by the element,

\[ \bar\kappa - l^2\,\Delta\bar\kappa = \kappa, \qquad \nabla\bar\kappa\cdot\boldsymbol{n} = 0 \text{ on the boundary}, \]
and the yield stress follows from the over-nonlocal combination ( \( m = 1 \): standard nonlocal, \( m > 1 \): over-nonlocal, \( m = 0 \): local, i.e., RankineSofteningPlasticity)
\[ \hat\kappa = m\,\bar\kappa + (1-m)\,\kappa, \qquad f_\mathrm{y}(\hat\kappa) = \max\left(f_\mathrm{y}^0 + H_\mathrm{iso,lin}\hat\kappa + \Delta f_\mathrm{y}^{0,\infty}\left(1-\exp(-\delta\hat\kappa)\right),\ 0\right). \]
For a softening law and \( m > 1 \), the yield stress increases with \( \kappa \) at a fixed nonlocal variable, so that the return mapping hardens locally and the plastic strain cannot localize in a set of measure zero. The yield stress is floored at zero in terms of \( \hat\kappa \); the stress power \( \boldsymbol{\sigma}: \Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^\mathrm{p} = f_\mathrm{y}(\hat\kappa)\,\Delta\kappa \) is the dissipation increment, the free energy is the elastic strain energy.

The backward Euler return mapping in principal space is that of RankineSofteningPlasticity with the hardening modulus \( (1-m)\,f_\mathrm{y}' \). Returned to the element: \( \kappa \) (KLocal), \( c = l^2 \), the consistent tangent \( \partial\boldsymbol{\sigma}/\partial\Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \), \( \partial\boldsymbol{\sigma}/\partial\bar\kappa = -\sum_A (\mathbb{C}_\mathrm{p}\,\partial\Delta\boldsymbol{ \lambda}/\partial\bar\kappa)_A\,\boldsymbol{n}_A\otimes\boldsymbol{n}_A \) with \( \partial\Delta\boldsymbol{ \lambda}/\partial\bar\kappa = -m\,f_\mathrm{y}'\,\boldsymbol{J}^{-1}\boldsymbol{1} \) on the active surfaces, \( \partial\kappa/\partial\Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} = \boldsymbol{1} - (\partial\boldsymbol{\sigma}/\partial \Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon})^T:\mathbb{C}^{-1}:\boldsymbol{1} \) and \( \partial\kappa/\partial\bar\kappa = \sum_A \partial\Delta\lambda_A/\partial\bar\kappa \). Since the yield stress depends on the nonlocal variable, a step without a strain increment may be plastic.

Material properties: \( E, \nu, f_\mathrm{y}^0, H_\mathrm{iso,lin}, \Delta f_\mathrm{y}^{0,\infty}, \delta, l, m \), optionally the density and the nonlocal viscosity (default zero, for explicit dynamics only).
