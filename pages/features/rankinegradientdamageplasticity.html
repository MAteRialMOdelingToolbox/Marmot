Gradient-enhanced Rankine damage-plasticity model

Implicit-gradient version of the Rankine damage-plasticity model: multi-surface Rankine plasticity in the effective stress with isotropic damage driven by a nonlocal variable, for the general gradient-enhanced displacement element.

Theory

The local damage driving variable \(\kappa_\mathrm{d}\), with \(\Delta\kappa_\mathrm{d} = \sum_A \Delta\lambda_A\) as in the Rankine damage-plasticity model, is the source of the Helmholtz equation solved by the element,

\[\bar\kappa_\mathrm{d} - l^2\,\Delta\bar\kappa_\mathrm{d} = \kappa_\mathrm{d}, \qquad \nabla\bar\kappa_\mathrm{d}\cdot\boldsymbol{n} = 0 \text{ on the boundary}.\]

The damage follows from the over-nonlocal combination

\[\hat\kappa = m\,\bar\kappa_\mathrm{d} + (1-m)\,\kappa_\mathrm{d}, \qquad \omega = \omega_\mathrm{max}\left[1 - \exp\left(-\frac{\hat\kappa_\mathrm{max}}{\omega_\mathrm{max}\, \varepsilon_\mathrm{f}}\right)\right], \qquad \hat\kappa_\mathrm{max} = \max_{\tau \le t} \hat\kappa(\tau), \qquad \boldsymbol{\sigma} = (1-\omega)\,\bar{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}.\]

The damage threshold \(\omega_\mathrm{max}\) bounds the damage without changing its initial slope, as in the Rankine damage-plasticity model. The history maximum keeps the damage irreversible also for \(m > 1\), where \(\hat\kappa\) may decrease. Since the effective stress does not depend on the damage, \(\kappa_\mathrm{d}\) does not depend on \(\bar\kappa_\mathrm{d}\).

Consistent Algorithmic Tangent Operator

\[\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\sigma}}{\partial\Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}} = (1-\omega)\,\bar{\mathbb{C}}^\mathrm{ep} - (1-m)\,\omega'\,\bar{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} \otimes \frac{\partial\kappa_\mathrm{d}}{\partial\Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \qquad \frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\sigma}}{\partial\bar\kappa_\mathrm{d}} = -m\,\omega'\,\bar{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}, \qquad \frac{\partial\kappa_\mathrm{d}}{\partial\bar\kappa_\mathrm{d}} = 0,\]

with \(\omega' = \exp\left(-\hat\kappa/(\omega_\mathrm{max}\,\varepsilon_\mathrm{f})\right)/\varepsilon_\mathrm{f}\) while \(\hat\kappa\) exceeds its history maximum, and zero otherwise.

The model is registered as RANKINEGRADIENTDAMAGEPLASTICITY in the gradient-enhanced hypoelastic factory with one nonlocal field.

Gradient-enhanced (implicit gradient) version of RankineDamagePlasticity: multi-surface Rankine plasticity in the effective stress with isotropic damage driven by a nonlocal variable, following the damage-plastic model of Grassl and Jirásek (2006, IJSS 43, 7166-7196) and the structure of GCDP.

The effective stress \( \bar{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} \) is bounded by the three Rankine surfaces of the perfectly plastic RankineSofteningPlasticity, and the local damage driving variable is the volumetric plastic strain \( \kappa_\mathrm{d} \) with \( \Delta\kappa_\mathrm{d} = \sum_A \Delta\lambda_A \), as in RankineDamagePlasticity. It is the source of the Helmholtz equation solved by the element,

\[ \bar\kappa_\mathrm{d} - l^2\,\Delta\bar\kappa_\mathrm{d} = \kappa_\mathrm{d}, \qquad \nabla\bar\kappa_\mathrm{d}\cdot\boldsymbol{n} = 0 \text{ on the boundary}, \]
and the damage follows from the over-nonlocal combination ( \( m = 1 \): standard nonlocal, \( m > 1 \): over-nonlocal, \( m = 0 \): local)
\[ \hat\kappa = m\,\bar\kappa_\mathrm{d} + (1-m)\,\kappa_\mathrm{d}, \qquad \omega = \omega_\mathrm{max} \left[1 - \exp\left(-\frac{\hat\kappa_\mathrm{max}}{\omega_\mathrm{max}\, \varepsilon_\mathrm{f}}\right)\right], \qquad \hat\kappa_\mathrm{max} = \max_{\tau \le t} \hat\kappa(\tau), \qquad \boldsymbol{\sigma} = (1-\omega)\,\bar{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}. \]
The damage threshold \( 0 < \omega_\mathrm{max} \le 1 \) bounds the damage without changing its initial slope ( \( \omega_\mathrm{max} = 1 \): exponential law \( \omega = 1 - \exp(-\hat\kappa_\mathrm{max}/ \varepsilon_\mathrm{f}) \)). The history maximum keeps the damage irreversible also for \( m > 1 \), where \( \hat\kappa \) may decrease. Since the effective stress does not depend on the damage, \( \kappa_\mathrm{d} \) does not depend on \( \bar\kappa_\mathrm{d} \). Free energy \( (1-\omega)\,\bar\psi_\mathrm{el} \) and dissipation increment \( (1-\omega)\,\bar{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}:\Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^\mathrm{p} + \bar\psi_\mathrm{el}\, \Delta\omega \) as in RankineDamagePlasticity.

Returned to the element: \( \kappa_\mathrm{d} \) (KLocal), \( c = l^2 \), and the tangents \( \partial\boldsymbol{\sigma}/\partial\Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} = (1-\omega)\,\bar{\mathbb{C}}^\mathrm{ep} - (1-m)\,\omega'\,\bar{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} \otimes \partial\kappa_\mathrm{d}/\partial\Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \), \( \partial\boldsymbol{\sigma}/\partial\bar\kappa_\mathrm{d} = -m\,\omega'\,\bar{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} \), \( \partial\kappa_\mathrm{d}/\partial\Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} = \boldsymbol{1} - \bar{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathrm{ep},T}:\mathbb{C}^{-1}:\boldsymbol{1} \) and \( \partial\kappa_\mathrm{d}/\partial \bar\kappa_\mathrm{d} = 0 \), with \( \omega' = \exp(-\hat\kappa/(\omega_\mathrm{max}\,\varepsilon_\mathrm{f}))/ \varepsilon_\mathrm{f} \) while \( \hat\kappa \) exceeds its history maximum, and zero otherwise.

Material properties: \( E, \nu, f_\mathrm{t}, \varepsilon_\mathrm{f}, \omega_\mathrm{max}, l, m \), optionally the density and the nonlocal viscosity (default zero, for explicit dynamics only).
