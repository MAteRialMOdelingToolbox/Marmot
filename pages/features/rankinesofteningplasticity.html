Rankine softening plasticity model

Multi-surface Rankine (maximum principal stress) plasticity with isotropic hardening/softening. The parameters and the hardening law are identical to the Von Mises model; softening is obtained by negative values of \(H_\mathrm{iso,lin}\) and/or \(\Delta f_\mathrm{y}^{0,\infty}\).

Theory

Yield Functions and Hardening Law

Three yield functions, one for each principal stress \(\sigma_A\), bound the admissible stress states

\[f_A\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma},\kappa\right) = \sigma_A - f_\mathrm{y}\left(\kappa\right) \leq 0, \qquad A=1,2,3,\]

with the yield stress

\[f_\mathrm{y}\left(\kappa\right) = \max\left(f_\mathrm{y}^0 + H_\mathrm{iso,lin}\kappa + \Delta f_\mathrm{y}^{0,\infty}\left(1-\exp\left(-\delta\kappa\right)\right),\ 0\right).\]

The yield stress is floored at zero: with linear softening, the material retains no tensile strength beyond \(\kappa = f_\mathrm{y}^0/|H_\mathrm{iso,lin}|\) and deforms without plastic work, while compressive principal stresses remain admissible. A positive residual strength \(f_\mathrm{y}^0 + \Delta f_\mathrm{y}^{0,\infty}\) follows from \(H_\mathrm{iso,lin} = 0\) and \(-f_\mathrm{y}^0 < \Delta f_\mathrm{y}^{0,\infty} < 0\).

Flow Rule

Plastic flow is associated, following Koiter’s rule for multi-surface plasticity

\[\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}^\mathrm{p} = \sum_{A} \dot{\lambda}_A \frac{\partial f_A}{\partial \boldsymbol{\sigma}} = \sum_{A} \dot{\lambda}_A\, \boldsymbol{n}_A\otimes\boldsymbol{n}_A,\]

where \(\boldsymbol{n}_A\) are the principal directions. The consistency parameters obey the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker conditions \(\dot{\lambda}_A f_A=0\), \(\dot{\lambda}_A\geq0\), \(f_A\leq0\).

The hardening variable evolves as

\[\dot{\kappa} = \sum_A \dot{\lambda}_A,\]

which renders the dissipated plastic work \(\boldsymbol{\sigma}:\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}^\mathrm{p}=f_\mathrm{y}\dot{\kappa}\), and \(\kappa=\varepsilon^\mathrm{p}_{11}\) under uniaxial tension.

Implementation

Stress Update Algorithm

The elastic trial stress \(\boldsymbol{\sigma}^\mathrm{trial}=\boldsymbol{\sigma}^n+\mathbf{C}^\mathrm{e}:\Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\) is decomposed spectrally. Due to isotropy, the principal directions are preserved by the backward Euler return mapping, which therefore reduces to principal space:

\[\sigma_A = \sigma_A^\mathrm{trial} - \sum_{B\in\mathcal{A}} C^\mathrm{e}_{AB}\Delta\lambda_B, \qquad C^\mathrm{e}_{AB} = \lambda + 2G\,\delta_{AB},\]

with the residuals \(r_A = \sigma_A - f_\mathrm{y}\left(\kappa^n + \sum_B\Delta\lambda_B\right) = 0\) for all surfaces in the active set \(\mathcal{A}\), solved by Newton’s method.

The active set is initialized with all surfaces violated by the trial stress. If the solution yields a negative \(\Delta\lambda_A\) or violates an inactive surface, the remaining combinations of active surfaces are tried, analogous to the CDP model.

Consistent Algorithmic Tangent Operator

The principal algorithmic tangent reads

\[D_{AB} = \frac{\partial\sigma_A}{\partial\varepsilon^\mathrm{trial}_B} = C^\mathrm{e}_{AB} - \sum_{C,D\in\mathcal{A}} C^\mathrm{e}_{AC}\left[C^\mathrm{e}_{CD} + \frac{\partial f_\mathrm{y}}{\partial\kappa}\right]^{-1} C^\mathrm{e}_{DB}.\]

The tangent of the isotropic tensor function \(\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^\mathrm{trial})\) follows as

\[\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\sigma}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}} = \sum_{A,B} D_{AB}\, \mathbf{E}_A\otimes\mathbf{E}_B + \sum_{A<B} 2\,c_{AB}\, \mathbf{M}_{AB}\otimes\mathbf{M}_{AB}, \qquad c_{AB} = 2G\,\frac{\sigma_A-\sigma_B}{\sigma^\mathrm{trial}_A-\sigma^\mathrm{trial}_B},\]

with \(\mathbf{E}_A=\boldsymbol{n}_A\otimes\boldsymbol{n}_A\) and \(\mathbf{M}_{AB}=\mathrm{sym}\left(\boldsymbol{n}_A\otimes\boldsymbol{n}_B\right)\). For repeated trial eigenvalues the limit \(c_{AB}=\frac{1}{2}\left(D_{AA}-D_{AB}+D_{BB}-D_{BA}\right)\) is used.

Multi-surface Rankine (maximum principal stress) plasticity with isotropic hardening/softening.

Three yield surfaces \( f_A = \sigma_A - f_\mathrm{y}(\kappa) \), one per principal stress, share the hardening law of VonMisesModel. The return mapping is performed in principal stress space with an active-set strategy; the consistent tangent is assembled from the spectral representation.

Material properties: \( E, \nu, f_\mathrm{y}^0, H_\mathrm{iso,lin}, \Delta f_\mathrm{y}^{0,\infty}, \delta \) and optionally the density.
