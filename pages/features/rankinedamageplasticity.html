Rankine damage-plasticity model

Multi-surface Rankine plasticity in the effective stress combined with isotropic damage, following the damage-plastic model of Grassl and Jirásek (2006). The effective stress is bounded by the perfectly plastic Rankine softening plasticity model; softening results from the damage only.

Theory

The effective stress \(\bar{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\) follows from perfect Rankine plasticity with the yield functions \(f_A = \bar\sigma_A - f_\mathrm{t} \leq 0\) and associated flow. The nominal stress and the damage read

\[\boldsymbol{\sigma} = (1-\omega)\,\bar{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}, \qquad \omega = \omega_\mathrm{max}\left[1 - \exp\left(-\frac{\kappa_\mathrm{d}}{\omega_\mathrm{max}\, \varepsilon_\mathrm{f}}\right)\right], \qquad \Delta\kappa_\mathrm{d} = \mathrm{tr}\,\Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^\mathrm{p} = \sum_A \Delta\lambda_A.\]

The damage threshold \(\omega_\mathrm{max}\) bounds the damage without changing its initial slope \(1/\varepsilon_\mathrm{f}\); \(\omega_\mathrm{max} = 1\) gives the exponential law \(\omega = 1 - \exp\left(-\kappa_\mathrm{d}/\varepsilon_\mathrm{f}\right)\). All principal plastic strain increments are nonnegative for the Rankine surfaces, hence \(\kappa_\mathrm{d}\) and \(\omega\) never decrease, and under uniaxial tension \(\sigma = (1-\omega)\,f_\mathrm{t}\) with \(\kappa_\mathrm{d} = \varepsilon^\mathrm{p}\), which tends to the residual strength \((1-\omega_\mathrm{max})\,f_\mathrm{t}\). \(\varepsilon_\mathrm{f}\) is a material parameter without regularization by the element size; for regularized softening use the Gradient-enhanced Rankine damage-plasticity model.

The free energy is \(\psi = (1-\omega)\,\bar\psi_\mathrm{el}\) with \(\bar\psi_\mathrm{el} = \frac12\bar{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}:\mathbb{C}^{-1}:\bar{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\), and the dissipation increment is \((1-\omega)\,\bar{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}:\Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^\mathrm{p} + \bar\psi_\mathrm{el}\,\Delta\omega\).

Consistent Algorithmic Tangent Operator

\[\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\sigma}}{\partial\Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}} = (1-\omega)\,\bar{\mathbb{C}}^\mathrm{ep} - \bar{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} \otimes \frac{\partial\omega}{\partial\Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}, \qquad \frac{\partial\kappa_\mathrm{d}}{\partial\Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}} = \boldsymbol{1} - \bar{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathrm{ep},T}:\mathbb{C}^{-1}:\boldsymbol{1},\]

with the algorithmic tangent \(\bar{\mathbb{C}}^\mathrm{ep}\) of the effective stress.

The model is registered as RANKINEDAMAGEPLASTICITY and, with the co-rotational Hughes-Winget wrapper wrapper, as RANKINEDAMAGEPLASTICITY/HUGHES-WINGET.

Multi-surface Rankine plasticity in the effective stress combined with isotropic damage, following the damage-plastic model of Grassl and Jirásek (2006, IJSS 43, 7166-7196).

The effective stress \( \bar{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} \) is bounded by the three Rankine surfaces \( f_A = \bar\sigma_A - f_\mathrm{t} \) of RankineSofteningPlasticity without hardening or softening (associated flow, perfect plasticity, \( \Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^\mathrm{p} = \sum_A \Delta\lambda_A \mathbf{E}_A \)). Softening results from the scalar damage only,

\[ \boldsymbol{\sigma} = (1-\omega)\,\bar{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}, \qquad \omega = \omega_\mathrm{max} \left[1 - \exp\left(-\frac{\kappa_\mathrm{d}}{\omega_\mathrm{max}\, \varepsilon_\mathrm{f}}\right)\right], \qquad \Delta\kappa_\mathrm{d} = \Delta\varepsilon^\mathrm{p}_\mathrm{V}/x_\mathrm{s} = \mathrm{tr}\,\Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^\mathrm{p} = \sum_A \Delta\lambda_A, \]
since all principal plastic strain increments are nonnegative for the Rankine surfaces (ductility measure \( x_\mathrm{s} = 1 \)). The damage threshold \( 0 < \omega_\mathrm{max} \le 1 \) bounds the damage without changing its initial slope \( 1/\varepsilon_\mathrm{f} \); \( \omega_\mathrm{max} = 1 \) gives the exponential law \( \omega = 1 - \exp(-\kappa_\mathrm{d}/\varepsilon_\mathrm{f}) \). Hence \( \kappa_\mathrm{d} \) and \( \omega \) never decrease, and under uniaxial tension \( \sigma = (1-\omega)\,f_\mathrm{t} \) with \( \kappa_\mathrm{d} = \varepsilon^\mathrm{p} \), approaching \( (1-\omega_\mathrm{max})\,f_\mathrm{t} \). The free energy is \( \psi = (1-\omega)\,\bar\psi_\mathrm{el} \) with \( \bar\psi_\mathrm{el} = \frac12\bar{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} :\mathbb{C}^{-1}:\bar{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} \), and the dissipation increment is \( (1-\omega)\, \bar{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}:\Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^\mathrm{p} + \bar\psi_\mathrm{el}\,\Delta\omega \) (backward Euler). The consistent tangent reads \( (1-\omega)\,\bar{\mathbb{C}}^\mathrm{ep} - \bar{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} \otimes \partial\omega/\partial\Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \) with \( \partial\kappa_\mathrm{d}/\partial\Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} = \boldsymbol{1} - \bar{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathrm{ep},T}:\mathbb{C}^{-1}:\boldsymbol{1} \).

Material properties: \( E, \nu, f_\mathrm{t}, \varepsilon_\mathrm{f}, \omega_\mathrm{max} \) and optionally the density. \( \varepsilon_\mathrm{f} \) is a material parameter (no regularization by the element size); \( \varepsilon_\mathrm{f} = \infty \) recovers perfect Rankine plasticity.
