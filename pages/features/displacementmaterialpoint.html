Displacement Material Point

Theory

The displacement material point is the carrier of the material state in the finite-strain displacement formulation of the material point method (MPM). It consumes any material of the Marmot::Materials::MaterialFiniteStrain interface and is hosted by a Displacement Cell (MPM) or owned by a Displacement Particle (RKPM). The general interfaces of material points and cells are described in MPM Cells and Material Points, those of the particles in Meshfree Particles.

Three configurations are distinguished: the undeformed configuration \(\boldsymbol{X}\), the intermediate configuration \(\boldsymbol{Y}\) (the last accepted state, i.e., the configuration at the beginning of the current increment) and the current configuration \(\boldsymbol{x}\). The deformation gradient is updated multiplicatively,

\[\boldsymbol{F} = \frac{\partial \boldsymbol{x}}{\partial \boldsymbol{X}} = \Delta\boldsymbol{F}\,\boldsymbol{F}_n, \qquad \Delta\boldsymbol{F} = \frac{\partial \boldsymbol{x}}{\partial \boldsymbol{Y}} = \boldsymbol{I} + \frac{\partial \Delta\boldsymbol{u}}{\partial \boldsymbol{Y}}, \qquad \boldsymbol{F}_n = \frac{\partial \boldsymbol{Y}}{\partial \boldsymbol{X}},\]

where the host supplies the displacement increment \(\Delta\boldsymbol{u}\) and its gradient with respect to \(\boldsymbol{Y}\) (incrementDeformation; the contributions are accumulated after prepareYourself has reset \(\Delta\boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{0}\), \(\Delta\boldsymbol{F} = \boldsymbol{I}\)). computeYourself evaluates the material with \(\boldsymbol{F}\) and provides

  • the Kirchhoff stress \(\boldsymbol{\tau}\) (response.S),

  • its derivative with respect to the increment (tangents.dS_dDeltaF),

    \[\frac{\partial \tau_{ij}}{\partial \Delta F_{kL}} = \frac{\partial \tau_{ij}}{\partial F_{kN}}\,F_{n,LN} ,\]

    which the hosts contract with the gradients \(\partial N_B/\partial Y_L\) of their shape functions.

On acceptance of the increment, \(\boldsymbol{u} \leftarrow \boldsymbol{u} + \Delta\boldsymbol{u}\) and \(\boldsymbol{F}_n \leftarrow \Delta\boldsymbol{F}\,\boldsymbol{F}_n\). The position reported to the host (getCoordinatesAtCenter) is \(\boldsymbol{Y} = \boldsymbol{X} + \boldsymbol{u}\), the volume (getVolumeUndeformed) and the density (getDensityUndeformed, from the material) refer to the undeformed configuration. Velocity and acceleration are stored, but set by the host (e.g. by a Newmark-beta scheme in the particles).

In plane strain, the in-plane increment is expanded to 3D (the out-of-plane components of \(\Delta\boldsymbol{F}\) and \(\boldsymbol{F}_n\) remain those of the identity), the material is evaluated by computePlaneStrain with the 3D deformation gradient, and the in-plane parts of \(\boldsymbol{\tau}\) and of its tangent are passed to the host. The 3D material point evaluates the material with the 3D deformation gradient; the call also goes through computePlaneStrain, whose default implementation in Marmot::Materials::MaterialFiniteStrain forwards to computeStress.

The material point has no properties of its own; the material is assigned by name and properties (assignMaterial). Initial conditions are not supported (setInitialCondition has no effect).

template<int nDim>
class DisplacementMaterialPoint : public Marmot::Discretizations::MaterialPoints::MaterialPoint

Inheritance diagram for Marmot::Discretizations::MaterialPoints::DisplacementMaterialPoint:

Collaboration diagram for Marmot::Discretizations::MaterialPoints::DisplacementMaterialPoint:

MPM material point of the finite-strain displacement formulation.

The material point carries the state of a Marmot::Materials::MaterialFiniteStrain and the kinematics of a total deformation gradient that is updated multiplicatively, increment by increment. Three configurations are distinguished: the undeformed configuration \( \boldsymbol{X} \), the intermediate configuration \( \boldsymbol{Y} \) (the last accepted state, i.e., the configuration at the beginning of the current increment) and the current configuration \( \boldsymbol{x} \). The deformation gradient is

\[ \boldsymbol{F} = \frac{\partial \boldsymbol{x}}{\partial \boldsymbol{X}} = \Delta\boldsymbol{F}\,\boldsymbol{F}_n, \qquad \Delta\boldsymbol{F} = \frac{\partial \boldsymbol{x}}{\partial \boldsymbol{Y}} = \boldsymbol{I} + \frac{\partial \Delta\boldsymbol{u}}{\partial \boldsymbol{Y}}, \qquad \boldsymbol{F}_n = \frac{\partial \boldsymbol{Y}}{\partial \boldsymbol{X}}, \]
where the host (a cell or a particle) supplies the displacement increment \( \Delta\boldsymbol{u} \) and its gradient with respect to \( \boldsymbol{Y} \) through incrementDeformation(). computeYourself() evaluates the material with \( \boldsymbol{F} \) and provides the Kirchhoff stress \( \boldsymbol{\tau} \) (response.S) and its derivative with respect to the increment \( \Delta\boldsymbol{F} \) (tangents.dS_dDeltaF),
\[ \frac{\partial \tau_{ij}}{\partial \Delta F_{kL}} = \frac{\partial \tau_{ij}}{\partial F_{kN}}\,F_{n,LN}. \]
acceptStateAndPosition() then sets \( \boldsymbol{u} \leftarrow \boldsymbol{u} + \Delta\boldsymbol{u} \) and \( \boldsymbol{F}_n \leftarrow \Delta\boldsymbol{F}\,\boldsymbol{F}_n \).

Internally, all kinematic quantities are stored in 3D; in 2D (plane strain) the out-of-plane components of \( \Delta\boldsymbol{F} \) and \( \boldsymbol{F}_n \) remain those of the identity. The volume and the density are those of the undeformed configuration.

Template Parameters:

nDim – Spatial dimension (2: plane strain, 3: 3D).

State of the material point in the state vector provided by the host.

Layout (all quantities 3D, regardless of nDim): total displacement \( \boldsymbol{u} \) (“displacement”), velocity, acceleration, the displacement increment of the current increment \( \Delta\boldsymbol{u} \) (“delta displacement”), the incremental deformation gradient \( \Delta\boldsymbol{F} \) (“delta deformation gradient”), the deformation gradient of the last accepted state \( \boldsymbol{F}_n \) (“deformation gradient”), the Kirchhoff stress (“stress”), followed by the state of the material (“begin of material state”).

Plane-strain displacement material point (registered as “Displacement/PlaneStrain”).

The in-plane increment is expanded to 3D (out-of-plane components of the identity), the material is evaluated by Marmot::Materials::MaterialFiniteStrain::computePlaneStrain() with the 3D deformation gradient, and the in-plane parts of \( \boldsymbol{\tau} \) and \( \partial\boldsymbol{\tau}/\partial\Delta\boldsymbol{F} \) are kept. The full 3D Kirchhoff stress is also written to the state “stress”.
