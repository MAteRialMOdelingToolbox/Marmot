Gradient-enhanced corotational wrapper

The wrapper makes a small-strain gradient-enhanced material, Marmot::Materials::MaterialGradientEnhancedHypoElastic<n>, usable as a finite-strain one, Marmot::Materials::MaterialGradientEnhancedFiniteStrain<n>, in the frame of the polar rotation. It is the wrapper for a material that recomputes its stress from total strains, such as Gradient-enhanced microplane model M7 (GM7), for which the Hughes-Winget wrapper is not objective: that one rotates the stress it hands over, and GM7 overwrites it.

Theory

The deformation gradient is split into the polar rotation and the right stretch,

\[\boldsymbol F = \boldsymbol R\,\boldsymbol U, \qquad \boldsymbol U = \boldsymbol C^{1/2}, \qquad \boldsymbol C = \boldsymbol F^T\boldsymbol F .\]

The wrapped material lives in the frame that rotates with \(\boldsymbol R\). It is driven by the increment of the logarithmic (Hencky) strain and returns the rotated Kirchhoff stress, which is rotated out:

\[\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon} = \ln\boldsymbol U^{(n+1)} - \ln\boldsymbol U^{(n)}, \qquad \ln\boldsymbol U = \tfrac12 \ln\boldsymbol C, \qquad \boldsymbol\tau = \boldsymbol R\,\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}\,\boldsymbol R^T .\]

  • A superposed rigid rotation changes \(\boldsymbol R\) only. Everything the wrapped material sees is unchanged, so the stress rotates exactly, whatever the material does with its state; its tensor-valued internal variables (the microplane state of GM7) live in the rotating frame and are objective as well.

  • The logarithmic strain increments sum to \(\ln\boldsymbol U\), independent of the path. Under a pure stretch the material sees \(\ln\lambda = (\lambda-1) + O((\lambda-1)^2)\): the small-strain model is recovered up to second order.

  • \(\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}\) is work-conjugate to \(\ln\boldsymbol U\) for an isotropic response, so the energy densities of the wrapped material are per unit reference volume and are passed through unchanged.

\(\boldsymbol C\) is the same for \(\boldsymbol F\) and for its mirror image, so the strain does not show that a material point is turned inside out: \(\boldsymbol F = -\boldsymbol 1\) would be a point without strain or stress, with a reflection for \(\boldsymbol R\). The wrapper therefore refuses a deformation gradient whose determinant is not positive (std::invalid_argument).

The nonlocal fields are handed over as they are, together with their increment since the last call. They are referred to the rotating frame too (the nonlocal strain of GM7 is the nonlocal counterpart of \(\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\)), and their balance is written in the reference configuration by the consumers, so they are invariant under a rigid rotation.

Tangent

The tangent is analytic and consistent,

\[\frac{\partial\tau_{ij}}{\partial F_{kL}} = \frac{\partial R_{ia}}{\partial F_{kL}}\,\hat\tau_{ab}\,R_{jb} + R_{ia}\,\hat\tau_{ab}\, \frac{\partial R_{jb}}{\partial F_{kL}} + R_{ia}R_{jb}\,\mathbb C_{abcd}\, \frac{\partial(\ln U)_{cd}}{\partial F_{kL}}, \qquad \frac{\partial\boldsymbol R}{\partial F_{kL}} = \Bigl(\frac{\partial\boldsymbol F}{\partial F_{kL}} - \boldsymbol R\,\frac{\partial\boldsymbol U}{\partial F_{kL}}\Bigr)\boldsymbol U^{-1},\]

with \(\mathbb C\) the algorithmic tangent of the wrapped material. The derivatives of the isotropic tensor functions \(\tfrac12\ln\boldsymbol C\) and \(\boldsymbol C^{1/2}\) follow from the spectral decomposition \(\boldsymbol C = \sum_a \lambda_a \boldsymbol N_a\otimes\boldsymbol N_a\) (Daleckii-Krein),

\[\frac{\partial f(\boldsymbol C)_{ij}}{\partial C_{IJ}} = \sum_{a,b} \theta_{ab}\, N_{a,i} N_{b,j} N_{a,I} N_{b,J}, \qquad \theta_{ab} = \frac{f(\lambda_a) - f(\lambda_b)}{\lambda_a - \lambda_b}, \quad \theta_{aa} = f'(\lambda_a),\]

which is continuous where eigenvalues coincide. The divided differences are evaluated without cancellation (\(\ln\): by log1p; the square root: \(1/(\sqrt{\lambda_a}+\sqrt{\lambda_b})\)). The explicit update, computeStressExplicit, skips the tangent. The tangent of the nonlocal variables is \(\partial\boldsymbol\tau/\partial\bar N_K = \boldsymbol R\,(\partial\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}/\partial\bar N_K)\, \boldsymbol R^T\) and \(\partial L_K/\partial\boldsymbol F = \partial L_K/\partial\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon} : \partial\ln\boldsymbol U/\partial\boldsymbol F\).

Usage

The wrapper is registered as GM7/COROTATIONAL in the Marmot::Registry::MaterialGradientEnhancedFiniteStrainFactory<6>. Its material properties are those of Gradient-enhanced microplane model M7 (GM7), unchanged; the wrapper consumes none. It drives the particles GradientEnhanced6FiniteStrain... of Gradient-Enhanced Finite-Strain Particles, e.g. in EdelweissMeshfree:

material = {"material": "GM7/COROTATIONAL", "properties": np.array([...])}
particle = MarmotParticleWrapper("GradientEnhanced6FiniteStrainSQCNIxNSNI/3D/Hexa", ..., material)

What is verified

testGM7Corotational.cpp (GM7 module) checks

  • the spectral derivative of \(\tfrac12\ln\boldsymbol C\) against central differences, for distinct and for repeated eigenvalues;

  • a rigid rotation superposed on every increment of an anisotropic stretch path that runs into softening: the Kirchhoff stress rotates to \(10^{-10}\) of its norm, the local driving forces stay unchanged;

  • a pure stretch against small-strain GM7: the difference is of second order in the strain (halving the strain divides it by four);

  • the analytic tangent (all four blocks) against central differences at a damaged, rotated state, and that the explicit update gives the same stress;

  • that density, nonlocal viscosity and micro-inertia of GM7 are forwarded and its state names resolved.

Decorator presenting a small-strain gradient-enhanced material as a finite-strain one, in the frame of the polar rotation.

The deformation gradient is decomposed into the polar rotation and the right stretch, \( \boldsymbol{F} = \boldsymbol{R}\,\boldsymbol{U} \), \( \boldsymbol{U} = \boldsymbol{C}^{1/2} \), \( \boldsymbol{C} = \boldsymbol{F}^T\boldsymbol{F} \). The wrapped material lives in the frame that rotates with \( \boldsymbol{R} \) (the frame of the material fibres; Green-Naghdi): it is driven by the increment of the logarithmic (Hencky) strain

\[ \Delta\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}} = \ln\boldsymbol{U}^{(n+1)} - \ln\boldsymbol{U}^{(n)}, \qquad \ln\boldsymbol{U} = \tfrac12\ln\boldsymbol{C}, \]
and its stress is read as the rotated Kirchhoff stress, which is rotated out,
\[ \boldsymbol{\tau} = \boldsymbol{R}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\,\boldsymbol{R}^T . \]

Why this frame. A superposed rigid rotation changes \( \boldsymbol{R} \) and leaves \( \boldsymbol{U} \) – and hence everything the wrapped material sees – unchanged, so the Kirchhoff stress rotates exactly, whatever the wrapped material does with its own state. That is what Marmot::Materials::GradientEnhancedHughesWingetWrapper cannot offer a material that recomputes its stress from total strains (GM7 does: its stress is the homogenized microplane stress of its stored strains, and the stress it is handed is overwritten); it rotates the stress it hands over, which such a material discards. Tensor-valued internal variables of the wrapped material (GM7’s microplane state, its stored strains) live in the rotating frame too, so they are objective as well.

Why the logarithmic strain. It sums to a total strain, \( \sum\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}} = \ln\boldsymbol{U} \), independent of the path, so a material that accumulates its strain stores the Hencky strain; and under a pure stretch \( \lambda \) it hands the material \( \ln\lambda = (\lambda-1) + O((\lambda- 1)^2) \), i.e. the wrapped model is recovered up to second order in the strain. The rotated Kirchhoff stress is the stress work-conjugate to \( \ln\boldsymbol{U} \) for an isotropic response, so the elastic and dissipated energies the wrapped material reports are densities per unit reference volume and are passed through unchanged.

The nonlocal fields are handed over as they are (see GradientEnhancedHypoElasticWrapper): they are referred to the rotating frame as well – for GM7, the nonlocal strain is the nonlocal counterpart of \( \hat{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}} \) – and their balance is written in the reference configuration, so they are invariant under a superposed rigid rotation.

Tangent. Consistent and analytic:

\[ \frac{\partial\tau_{ij}}{\partial F_{kL}} = \frac{\partial R_{ia}}{\partial F_{kL}}\,\hat\tau_{ab}\,R_{jb} + R_{ia}\,\hat\tau_{ab}\, \frac{\partial R_{jb}}{\partial F_{kL}} + R_{ia}R_{jb}\,\mathbb{C}_{abcd}\, \frac{\partial(\ln U)_{cd}}{\partial F_{kL}}, \qquad \frac{\partial\boldsymbol{R}}{\partial F_{kL}} = \Bigl(\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{F}}{\partial F_{kL}} - \boldsymbol{R}\,\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{U}}{\partial F_{kL}}\Bigr)\boldsymbol{U}^{-1}, \]
with \( \mathbb{C} \) the algorithmic tangent of the wrapped material and the derivatives of the isotropic tensor functions \( \ln\boldsymbol{U} = \tfrac12\ln\boldsymbol{C} \) and \( \boldsymbol{U} = \boldsymbol{C}^{1/2} \) from the spectral decomposition of \( \boldsymbol{C} \) (spectralDerivative()). It is exact for any wrapped material whose stress does not depend on the stress it is handed (GM7), and for an incremental one too, since the stress it is handed is the rotated stress of the last call, which does not depend on \( \boldsymbol{F} \). The explicit path, computeStressExplicit(), skips it.

What it refuses. \( \boldsymbol{C} = \boldsymbol{F}^T\boldsymbol{F} \) is the same for \( \boldsymbol{F} \) and for its mirror image, so the strain does not show that a material point is turned inside out ( \( \det\boldsymbol{F} < 0 \); \( \boldsymbol{F} = -\boldsymbol{1} \) would be a point without strain or stress, with a reflection for \( \boldsymbol{R} \)). A deformation gradient without a positive determinant is refused (std::invalid_argument).

The state of the wrapper: \( \ln\boldsymbol{U} \) and \( \hat{\boldsymbol{\tau}} \) of the last call (both in Voigt notation, the strain with engineering shear), the nonlocal fields of the last call, and the state of the wrapped material. A zero state is the undeformed, stress-free one, so no initialization beyond the wrapped material’s is needed.

Inheritance diagram for Marmot::Materials::GradientEnhancedHypoElasticWrapper:

Collaboration diagram for Marmot::Materials::GradientEnhancedHypoElasticWrapper:

Common base of the decorators presenting a small-strain gradient-enhanced material as a finite-strain one.

What the decorators share: they own the wrapped material, keep its state variables in the slot materialstate of their own layout, forward its density, nonlocal viscosity and micro-inertia, resolve state names they do not know themselves in the wrapped material, and hand it the nonlocal fields together with their increment. The finite-strain interface passes the TOTAL nonlocal fields, while the wrapped material wants the fields and their increment; the increment is formed against the value of the last call, which each decorator keeps in a slot of its own layout (baseMaterialIncrement() below). The kinematics – which strain increment the wrapped material is driven by, and which stress it returns – is the derived class’ own: GradientEnhancedHughesWingetWrapper (an objective rate, for a material incremental in stress) and GradientEnhancedCorotationalWrapper (the logarithmic strain in the frame of the polar rotation, for any material).

Each derived class builds its layout in its constructor: its own slots, a slot for the nonlocal fields of the last call, and baseMaterialSlot, of size baseMaterial->getNumberOfRequiredStateVars().
