Voigt Notation

Symmetric second order tensors, such as the stress tensor \(\sig\) and the linearized strain tensor \(\eps\), are stored in Marmot as column vectors, and the fourth order tensors relating them as matrices. This page defines the component ordering and the scaling factors that are used consistently throughout the library.

Warning

Marmot orders the shear components as 12, 13, 23 (Abaqus convention). This differs from the ordering 23, 13, 12 found in much of the continuum mechanics literature and in several other finite element codes. When porting material parameters, stiffness matrices or state variable layouts from another code, check the shear ordering first — a mismatch swaps \(G_{12}\) and \(G_{23}\) and produces results that look plausible but are wrong.

Component ordering

The vector length follows from the spatial dimension as \(n_\mathrm{Voigt} = (n^2 + n)/2\), provided by the macro VOIGTFROMDIM. The orderings are

For the 3D case the mapping between the tensor indices \((i,j)\) and the Voigt index is

\[\begin{split}\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 3 & 4 \\ 3 & 1 & 5 \\ 4 & 5 & 2 \end{bmatrix}\end{split}\]

and is available in both directions as toVoigt<nDim>(i, j) and fromVoigt<nDim>(ij) in the namespace Marmot::Math::Tensors::IndexNotation. Prefer these helpers over hard-coded indices — code written against them stays correct if the ordering ever changes.

Stress and strain

Stress components are stored as they appear in the tensor, whereas the shear components of the strain are stored as engineering shear strains, i.e. twice the tensor components:

\[\begin{split}\sig^\mathrm{Voigt} &= \begin{bmatrix} \sigma_{11} & \sigma_{22} & \sigma_{33} & \sigma_{12} & \sigma_{13} & \sigma_{23} \end{bmatrix}^\mathsf{T} \\[1ex] \eps^\mathrm{Voigt} &= \begin{bmatrix} \varepsilon_{11} & \varepsilon_{22} & \varepsilon_{33} & \gamma_{12} & \gamma_{13} & \gamma_{23} \end{bmatrix}^\mathsf{T} = \begin{bmatrix} \varepsilon_{11} & \varepsilon_{22} & \varepsilon_{33} & 2\,\varepsilon_{12} & 2\,\varepsilon_{13} & 2\,\varepsilon_{23} \end{bmatrix}^\mathsf{T}\end{split}\]

This asymmetry is what makes the inner product \(\sig^\mathrm{Voigt} \cdot \eps^\mathrm{Voigt}\) equal to the strain energy density \(\sig : \eps\). It also means stress and strain vectors must never be converted with the same routine. The dedicated conversions are

Function

Purpose

stressToVoigt, voigtToStress

Tensor \(\leftrightarrow\) Voigt for stress-like quantities (shear copied directly)

strainToVoigt, voigtToStrain

Tensor \(\leftrightarrow\) Voigt for strain-like quantities (shear scaled by 2 resp. 1/2)

stressMatrixFromVoigt<nDim>, voigtFromStrainMatrix<nDim>

Dimension-templated variants for the 1D, 2D and 3D representations

The scaling vectors \(\mathbf{P} = [1, 1, 1, 2, 2, 2]^\mathsf{T}\) and \(\mathbf{P}^{-1} = [1, 1, 1, \tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{1}{2}]^\mathsf{T}\) are provided for converting between the two scalings. The constants \(\mathbf{I} = [1, 1, 1, 0, 0, 0]^\mathsf{T}\), \(\mathbf{I}_\mathrm{hyd}\) and the deviatoric projector \(\mathbf{I}_\mathrm{dev}\) are defined in the same namespace.

Fourth order tensors

A stiffness tensor in Voigt notation is a \(6\times6\) matrix that maps the engineering strain vector to the stress vector,

\[\sig^\mathrm{Voigt} = \Cel^\mathrm{Voigt}\, \eps^\mathrm{Voigt},\]

so that no additional factors of two appear when the tangent is used. As a consequence, the diagonal shear entries of an orthotropic stiffness matrix are the shear moduli themselves,

\[\Cel^\mathrm{Voigt}_{44} = G_{12}, \qquad \Cel^\mathrm{Voigt}_{55} = G_{13}, \qquad \Cel^\mathrm{Voigt}_{66} = G_{23},\]

using one-based matrix indices, i.e. entries (3,3), (4,4) and (5,5) in the zero-based C++ code. The corresponding compliance matrix holds \(1/G_{12}\), \(1/G_{13}\) and \(1/G_{23}\) in the same positions.

Conversion to and from the full fourth order tensor is available as voigtToStiffness and stiffnessToVoigt. These populate

\[\Cel_{ijkl} = \Cel^\mathrm{Voigt}_{\,\mathrm{toVoigt}(i,j)\,,\,\mathrm{toVoigt}(k,l)},\]

without halving the shear entries: summing over both \(kl\) and \(lk\) reproduces the factor of two that the engineering shear strain carries in the Voigt product.

Coordinate transformations

Because stress and strain use different shear scalings, they also require different transformation matrices under a change of basis. Both are built from a local coordinate system in Marmot::ContinuumMechanics::Voigt::Transformations:

  • transformationMatrixStressVoigt — for stress-like Voigt vectors

  • transformationMatrixStrainVoigt — for strain-like Voigt vectors; identical to the stress variant except that the upper right \(3\times3\) block is halved and the lower left block doubled

A stiffness matrix is rotated from a local material frame into the global frame as

\[\Cel^\mathrm{global} = \mathbf{T}_\sigma^{-1}\; \Cel^\mathrm{local}\; \mathbf{T}_\varepsilon,\]

which is how the transversely isotropic and orthotropic variants of the Linear Elastic model model are assembled.

Reduced representations

Plane and axisymmetric states are stored in shortened vectors, and the conversions to and from the full 3D vector are position-based rather than value-based:

Function

Mapping

voigtToPlaneVoigt

takes entries 0, 1 and 3 of the 3D vector (\(11\), \(22\), \(12\))

planeVoigtToVoigt

writes them back; the remaining entries are set to zero

voigtToAxisymmetricVoigt

takes entries 0 to 3 (\(rr\), \(zz\), \(\theta\theta\), \(rz\))

axisymmetricVoigtToVoigt

writes them back; the two out-of-plane shear entries are set to zero

Note that the in-plane shear sits at index 3 of the 3D vector but at index 2 of the plane vector, which is why the plane-strain and plane-stress tangent reductions in Marmot::ContinuumMechanics::PlaneStrain and Marmot::ContinuumMechanics::PlaneStress pick row and column 3 of the \(6\times6\) tangent.

Note

planeVoigtToVoigt assumes that the out-of-plane components are zero. It must not be used to restore a state in which \(\sigma_{33}\) is non-zero, such as a plane strain stress state.

Strain-displacement operators

The B-operators in Marmot::Discretizations::Elements produce strain vectors in exactly this ordering. For the 3D case,

\[\begin{split}\mathbf{B}_a = \begin{bmatrix} \partial N_a / \partial x_1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & \partial N_a / \partial x_2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \partial N_a / \partial x_3 \\ \partial N_a / \partial x_2 & \partial N_a / \partial x_1 & 0 \\ \partial N_a / \partial x_3 & 0 & \partial N_a / \partial x_1 \\ 0 & \partial N_a / \partial x_3 & \partial N_a / \partial x_2 \end{bmatrix}\end{split}\]

where the last three rows again correspond to \(\gamma_{12}\), \(\gamma_{13}\) and \(\gamma_{23}\). The same ordering is used by the B-bar operator, the Green-Lagrange operator and the enhanced assumed strain interpolations.

Implementation

The definitions above live in MarmotVoigt.h and MarmotTensor.h; see MarmotMechanicsCore and MarmotMathCore for the full interface.
