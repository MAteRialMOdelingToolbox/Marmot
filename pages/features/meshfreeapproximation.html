Meshfree Approximation

Overview

The RKPM particles (Meshfree Particles) take their shape functions from a meshfree approximation: a set of kernel functions \(\phi_A\), one per node \(A\) with center \(\boldsymbol{x}_A\), is corrected such that polynomials up to a given completeness order \(n\) are reproduced exactly. MarmotMeshfreeCore provides

  • the interface Marmot::Discretizations::Meshfree::KernelFunction and the boxed tensor-product B-spline kernels of 2nd and 3rd order,

  • the interface Marmot::Discretizations::Meshfree::Approximation and the reproducing kernel approximation with explicit (Marmot::Discretizations::Meshfree::ReproducingKernelApproximation) and implicit (Marmot::Discretizations::Meshfree::ReproducingKernelApproximationImplicit) gradients,

  • the complete monomial basis (namespace Marmot::Math),

  • B-spline basis functions of arbitrary degree (MarmotBSpline.h), used by the B-spline cell geometries of MPM Cells and Material Points.

An approximation is evaluated at a point \(\boldsymbol{x}\) for a list of \(n_\mathrm{c}\) candidate kernels. It returns the values \(\Psi_A(\boldsymbol{x})\) (an array of length \(n_\mathrm{c}\)) and the gradients as a column-major \(d \times n_\mathrm{c}\) array (entry \(i + d\,A\) holds \(\partial\Psi_A/\partial x_i\)), both indexed by the position of the kernel in the candidate list. Candidates that do not cover \(\boldsymbol{x}\) get zero entries. Whether \(\boldsymbol{x}\) and \(\boldsymbol{x}_A\) are reference or current coordinates is decided by the caller (the particle); the approximation only uses their difference.

Theory

Reproducing kernel approximation

The reproducing kernel shape function of node \(A\) is its kernel multiplied by a correction function,

\[\Psi_A(\boldsymbol{x}) = \boldsymbol{H}^T(\boldsymbol{x} - \boldsymbol{x}_A)\,\boldsymbol{b}(\boldsymbol{x})\, \phi_A(\boldsymbol{x}),\]

where \(\boldsymbol{H}\) is the vector of all monomials of total degree \(\le n\) (see Monomial basis), evaluated at the shifted coordinates \(\boldsymbol{x} - \boldsymbol{x}_A\). The coefficients \(\boldsymbol{b}\) follow from the moment matrix \(\boldsymbol{M}\),

\[\boldsymbol{M}(\boldsymbol{x})\,\boldsymbol{b}(\boldsymbol{x}) = \boldsymbol{H}_0, \qquad \boldsymbol{M}(\boldsymbol{x}) = \sum_B \boldsymbol{H}(\boldsymbol{x} - \boldsymbol{x}_B)\, \boldsymbol{H}^T(\boldsymbol{x} - \boldsymbol{x}_B)\,\phi_B(\boldsymbol{x}), \qquad \boldsymbol{H}_0 = \boldsymbol{H}(\boldsymbol{0}) = [1, 0, \dots, 0]^T,\]

with the sum over the kernels covering \(\boldsymbol{x}\) (those with \(|\phi_B(\boldsymbol{x})| > 10^{-14}\)). Since \(\boldsymbol{M}\) is symmetric, this is the familiar form \(\Psi_A = \boldsymbol{H}^T(\boldsymbol{0})\,\boldsymbol{M}^{-1}\boldsymbol{H}(\boldsymbol{x} - \boldsymbol{x}_A)\,\phi_A\). By construction, \(\sum_A \Psi_A(\boldsymbol{x})\,\boldsymbol{H}(\boldsymbol{x} - \boldsymbol{x}_A) = \boldsymbol{H}_0\), i.e., the reproducing conditions

\[\sum_A \Psi_A(\boldsymbol{x})\,(\boldsymbol{x} - \boldsymbol{x}_A)^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} = \delta_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\boldsymbol{0}} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sum_A \Psi_A(\boldsymbol{x})\,\boldsymbol{x}_A^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} = \boldsymbol{x}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}, \qquad |\boldsymbol{\alpha}| \le n,\]

hold for all multi-indices \(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\). In particular, the shape functions form a partition of unity (\(\boldsymbol{\alpha} = \boldsymbol{0}\)) and reproduce linear fields for \(n \ge 1\). The completeness order \(n\) is thus the order of the reproduced polynomials, while the continuity of \(\Psi_A\) is that of the kernels (\(C^1\) or \(C^2\) for the kernels below), since \(\boldsymbol{H}\) is smooth and \(\boldsymbol{M}\) inherits the continuity of the kernels. The shape functions are in general not interpolating, \(\Psi_A(\boldsymbol{x}_B) \ne \delta_{AB}\). All linear systems are solved with a column-pivoting Householder QR decomposition of \(\boldsymbol{M}\), computed once per evaluation point.

Reduction of the completeness order. \(\boldsymbol{M}\) is singular if fewer kernels cover \(\boldsymbol{x}\) than \(\boldsymbol{H}\) has entries. At every evaluation point, the requested order is therefore reduced until the number of covering kernels is at least \(\binom{d+n}{d}\) (the size of \(\boldsymbol{H}\)). This is a necessary condition only; degenerate node arrangements (e.g., all covering nodes on a line in 2D) can still give a singular moment matrix, so the support radius must be chosen large enough for the requested order.

Explicit gradients

Marmot::Discretizations::Meshfree::ReproducingKernelApproximation computes the exact derivatives of \(\Psi_A\) by the product rule, with \((\bullet)_{,i} = \partial(\bullet)/\partial x_i\),

\[\Psi_{A,i} = \left(\boldsymbol{b}_{,i}^T\boldsymbol{H} + \boldsymbol{b}^T\boldsymbol{H}_{,i}\right)\phi_A + \boldsymbol{b}^T\boldsymbol{H}\,\phi_{A,i}, \qquad \boldsymbol{b}_{,i} = -\boldsymbol{M}^{-1}\boldsymbol{M}_{,i}\,\boldsymbol{b},\]
\[\boldsymbol{M}_{,i} = \sum_B \left(\boldsymbol{H}_{,i}\boldsymbol{H}^T + \boldsymbol{H}\boldsymbol{H}_{,i}^T\right) \phi_B + \boldsymbol{H}\boldsymbol{H}^T\phi_{B,i},\]

where \(\boldsymbol{H}\) and \(\boldsymbol{H}_{,i}\) are evaluated at \(\boldsymbol{x} - \boldsymbol{x}_A\) (resp. \(\boldsymbol{x} - \boldsymbol{x}_B\)). The \(d\) vectors \(\boldsymbol{b}_{,i}\) are computed once per point and reused for all nodes. These gradients are the derivatives of the values, and they reproduce the derivatives of the polynomials up to order \(n\).

Implicit gradients

Marmot::Discretizations::Meshfree::ReproducingKernelApproximationImplicit returns the same values \(\Psi_A\), but constructs the gradients directly as corrected kernels (implicit, or synchronized, gradients),

\[\Psi^{(i)}_A(\boldsymbol{x}) = \boldsymbol{H}^T(\boldsymbol{x} - \boldsymbol{x}_A)\, \boldsymbol{b}^{(i)}(\boldsymbol{x})\,\phi_A(\boldsymbol{x}), \qquad \boldsymbol{M}(\boldsymbol{x})\,\boldsymbol{b}^{(i)}(\boldsymbol{x}) = \boldsymbol{H}^{(i)}_0, \qquad \boldsymbol{H}^{(i)}_0 = -\left.\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{H}(\boldsymbol{z})}{\partial z_i}\right|_{\boldsymbol{z} = \boldsymbol{0}}.\]

\(\boldsymbol{H}^{(i)}_0\) is \(-1\) at the position of the linear monomial \(z_i\) in \(\boldsymbol{H}\) and zero elsewhere; the negative sign stems from the shifted argument \(\boldsymbol{z} = \boldsymbol{x} - \boldsymbol{x}_A\). The position is located from the gradient of the basis at the origin, so it is correct for every completeness order and dimension (for \(n \ge 2\) the linear monomials are not the entries \(1, \dots, d\) of \(\boldsymbol{H}\), see Monomial basis). This enforces the gradient reproducing conditions

\[\sum_A \Psi^{(i)}_A(\boldsymbol{x})\,\boldsymbol{x}_A^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} = \frac{\partial\boldsymbol{x}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}{\partial x_i}, \qquad |\boldsymbol{\alpha}| \le n.\]

The implicit gradients reproduce the derivatives of all polynomials up to order \(n\), like the explicit ones, but they are not the derivatives of \(\Psi_A\). They need neither kernel gradients nor \(\boldsymbol{M}_{,i}\): the \(1 + d\) right-hand sides \([\boldsymbol{H}_0, \boldsymbol{H}^{(1)}_0, \dots, \boldsymbol{H}^{(d)}_0]\) are solved with a single decomposition of \(\boldsymbol{M}\).

In both classes, only computeShapeFunctions and computeShapeFunctionsAndGradients are implemented; computeShapeFunctionGradients throws std::runtime_error.

Kernel functions

Both kernels are boxed tensor products of a one-dimensional B-spline \(w\) with support radius \(a\),

\[\phi_A(\boldsymbol{x}) = \prod_{i=1}^{d} w(x_i - x_{A,i}), \qquad z = \frac{|r|}{a},\]

The gradients follow by the product rule, \(\partial\phi_A/\partial x_i = w'(x_i - x_{A,i})\prod_{j\ne i} w(x_j - x_{A,j})\). The kernels are not normalized; a constant scaling of the kernel cancels in \(\Psi_A\).

The support is the open box \(|x_i - x_{A,i}| < a\) in every direction, i.e., the support radius \(a\) is half the edge length of the box (not the radius of a sphere) and equal in all directions; isInSupport tests \(\phi_A > 0\), and getBoundingBox returns \([\boldsymbol{x}_A - a, \boldsymbol{x}_A + a]\). With a node spacing \(h\), a support radius \(a\) covers about \(2a/h\) nodes per direction; the unit tests use \(a = 1.6\,h\) for \(n = 1\) and \(a = 2.4\,h\) for \(n = 2\).

A kernel does not own its center: it keeps the pointer passed to its constructor, and moveTo overwrites the coordinates behind that pointer. The storage must outlive the kernel.

Monomial basis

Marmot::Math::computeMonomialBasis evaluates the complete basis of all monomials \(x_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots x_d^{\alpha_d}\) with \(\alpha_1 + \dots + \alpha_d \le n\); it has computeSizeOfMonomialBasisVector(n, d) \(= \binom{d+n}{d}\) entries. B-spline basis functions

MarmotBSpline.h provides the B-spline basis function \(N_{i,p}(u)\) of degree \(p\) over a non-decreasing knot vector \(\{z_0, z_1, \dots\}\) (B<p>(u, knotVec, i)) and its derivative (dB_dU<p>(u, knotVec, i)), evaluated by the Cox–de Boor recursion,

\[\begin{split}N_{i,0}(u) = \begin{cases} 1, & z_i \le u < z_{i+1}, \\ 0, & \text{otherwise}, \end{cases} \qquad N_{i,p}(u) = \frac{u - z_i}{z_{i+p} - z_i}N_{i,p-1}(u) + \frac{z_{i+p+1} - u}{z_{i+p+1} - z_{i+1}}N_{i+1,p-1}(u),\end{split}\]

where a term with a knot difference below \(10^{-14}\) (repeated knots) is omitted. The degree is a template parameter, so the recursion is resolved at compile time. These functions are declared in the Marmot::Math namespace. They are the basis of the B-spline cell geometries of the material point method (MPM Cells and Material Points); they are independent of the kernels above.

Usage

The approximation is not registered with a factory; the host constructs it directly. In EdelweissMeshfree the approximation types ReproducingKernel and ReproducingKernelImplicitGradient (argument completenessOrder) and the kernel type BSplineBoxed (arguments supportRadius and continuityOrder = 2 or 3, selecting the 2nd or 3rd order kernel) map to the classes of this page; see Meshfree methods for the overall workflow.

class KernelFunction

Inheritance diagram for Marmot::Discretizations::Meshfree::KernelFunction:

Abstract interface of a meshfree kernel (window) function \( \phi_A(\boldsymbol{x}) \).

A kernel function is attached to a node (particle) \( A \) with center \( \boldsymbol{x}_A \) and has compact support. The reproducing kernel approximation (ReproducingKernelApproximation) builds its shape functions \( \Psi_A \) by correcting these kernels such that polynomials up to a given order are reproduced exactly; the continuity of \( \Psi_A \) is inherited from the kernel.

All coordinates are passed as raw arrays of length \( d \) (the spatial dimension of the kernel). The configuration in which the coordinates are given (reference or current) is decided by the caller; the kernel only compares them with its center coordinates.

class KernelFunctionBSpline2ndOrderBoxed : public Marmot::Discretizations::Meshfree::KernelFunction

Inheritance diagram for Marmot::Discretizations::Meshfree::KernelFunctionBSpline2ndOrderBoxed:

Collaboration diagram for Marmot::Discretizations::Meshfree::KernelFunctionBSpline2ndOrderBoxed:

Boxed tensor-product kernel from 2nd order (quadratic) B-splines.

The kernel of node \( A \) with center \( \boldsymbol{x}_A \) and support radius \( a \) is the tensor product of one-dimensional 2nd order (quadratic) B-splines \( w \) in each coordinate direction,

\[ \phi_A(\boldsymbol{x}) = \prod_{i=1}^{d} w\left( x_i - x_{A,i} \right), \]
with
\[\begin{split} w(r) = \begin{cases} 1 - 2 z^2, & 0 \le z \le \tfrac{1}{2}, \\ 2 (1 - z)^2, & \tfrac{1}{2} < z \le 1, \\ 0, & z > 1, \end{cases} \qquad z = \frac{|r|}{a}. \end{split}\]
\( w \) is the quadratic B-spline on the knots \( \{-a, -a/2, a/2, a\} \), scaled to \( w(0) = 1 \); it is \( C^1 \)-continuous, so the kernel and the resulting reproducing kernel shape functions are \( C^1 \).

The support is the open box \( |x_i - x_{A,i}| < a \), \( i = 1, \dots, d \) (hence “boxed”); its bounding box is \( [\boldsymbol{x}_A - a, \boldsymbol{x}_A + a] \). The support radius \( a \) is the half edge length of the box, identical in all directions, and the kernel is not normalized (the reproducing kernel correction removes any constant scaling of the kernel).

Note

The kernel does not own its center coordinates: it stores the pointer passed to the constructor, and moveTo() writes the new center through this pointer. The pointed-to storage must outlive the kernel.

class Approximation

Inheritance diagram for Marmot::Discretizations::Meshfree::Approximation:

Abstract interface of a meshfree approximation, which evaluates shape functions from a set of kernels.

Given an evaluation point \( \boldsymbol{x} \) and a list of \( n \) candidate kernel functions \( \phi_A \), \( A = 0, \dots, n-1 \) (KernelFunction), an approximation computes the shape functions \( \Psi_A(\boldsymbol{x}) \) and/or their gradients. The output arrays are indexed by the position of the kernel in the candidate list; candidates that do not cover \( \boldsymbol{x} \) get zero entries.

The gradient output is a column-major \( d \times n \) array, i.e., entry \( i + d\,A \) holds \( \partial \Psi_A / \partial x_i \), where \( d \) is the spatial dimension.

Subclassed by Marmot::Discretizations::Meshfree::ReproducingKernelApproximation

class ReproducingKernelApproximation : public Marmot::Discretizations::Meshfree::Approximation

Inheritance diagram for Marmot::Discretizations::Meshfree::ReproducingKernelApproximation:

Collaboration diagram for Marmot::Discretizations::Meshfree::ReproducingKernelApproximation:

Reproducing kernel (RK) approximation with explicit (direct) shape function gradients.

The shape function of node \( A \) is the kernel \( \phi_A \) (KernelFunction) multiplied by a correction function,

\[ \Psi_A(\boldsymbol{x}) = \boldsymbol{H}^T(\boldsymbol{x} - \boldsymbol{x}_A)\, \boldsymbol{b}(\boldsymbol{x})\, \phi_A(\boldsymbol{x}), \]
where \( \boldsymbol{H} \) is the vector of all monomials up to the completeness order \( n \) (Math::computeMonomialBasis(), first entry \( 1 \)) evaluated at the shifted coordinates \( \boldsymbol{x} - \boldsymbol{x}_A \). The coefficient vector \( \boldsymbol{b} \) solves
\[ \boldsymbol{M}(\boldsymbol{x})\, \boldsymbol{b}(\boldsymbol{x}) = \boldsymbol{H}_0, \qquad \boldsymbol{M}(\boldsymbol{x}) = \sum_{B} \boldsymbol{H}(\boldsymbol{x} - \boldsymbol{x}_B)\, \boldsymbol{H}^T(\boldsymbol{x} - \boldsymbol{x}_B)\, \phi_B(\boldsymbol{x}), \qquad \boldsymbol{H}_0 = \boldsymbol{H}(\boldsymbol{0}) = [1, 0, \dots, 0]^T, \]
with the moment matrix \( \boldsymbol{M} \) summed over the kernels covering \( \boldsymbol{x} \). This enforces the reproducing conditions \( \sum_A \Psi_A(\boldsymbol{x})\, (\boldsymbol{x} - \boldsymbol{x}_A)^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} = \delta_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\boldsymbol{0}} \), or equivalently \( \sum_A \Psi_A(\boldsymbol{x})\, \boldsymbol{x}_A^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} = \boldsymbol{x}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} \), for all multi-indices \( |\boldsymbol{\alpha}| \le n \); in particular, the shape functions form a partition of unity. The continuity of \( \Psi_A \) is that of the kernels.

The gradients are the exact derivatives of \( \Psi_A \) (product rule),

\[ \Psi_{A,i} = \left( \boldsymbol{b}_{,i}^T \boldsymbol{H} + \boldsymbol{b}^T \boldsymbol{H}_{,i} \right) \phi_A + \boldsymbol{b}^T \boldsymbol{H}\, \phi_{A,i}, \qquad \boldsymbol{b}_{,i} = -\boldsymbol{M}^{-1} \boldsymbol{M}_{,i}\, \boldsymbol{b}, \qquad \boldsymbol{M}_{,i} = \sum_B \left( \boldsymbol{H}_{,i} \boldsymbol{H}^T + \boldsymbol{H} \boldsymbol{H}_{,i}^T \right) \phi_B + \boldsymbol{H} \boldsymbol{H}^T \phi_{B,i}, \]
with \( (\bullet)_{,i} = \partial (\bullet) / \partial x_i \) and \( \boldsymbol{H} \) evaluated at \( \boldsymbol{x} - \boldsymbol{x}_A \) (resp. \( \boldsymbol{x} - \boldsymbol{x}_B \)). The linear systems are solved with a column-pivoting Householder QR decomposition of \( \boldsymbol{M} \), which is computed once per evaluation point.

If fewer kernels cover \( \boldsymbol{x} \) than the basis has entries, \( \boldsymbol{M} \) is singular; the completeness order is then reduced for this evaluation point (getCorrectedCompletenessOrder()).

See ReproducingKernelApproximationImplicit for the variant with implicit gradients.

class ReproducingKernelApproximationImplicit : public Marmot::Discretizations::Meshfree::ReproducingKernelApproximation

Inheritance diagram for Marmot::Discretizations::Meshfree::ReproducingKernelApproximationImplicit:

Collaboration diagram for Marmot::Discretizations::Meshfree::ReproducingKernelApproximationImplicit:

Reproducing kernel approximation with implicit gradients.

The shape function values are those of ReproducingKernelApproximation. The gradients are not obtained by differentiating \( \Psi_A \) but are constructed directly as corrected kernels (implicit, or synchronized, gradients),

\[ \Psi^{(i)}_A(\boldsymbol{x}) = \boldsymbol{H}^T(\boldsymbol{x} - \boldsymbol{x}_A)\, \boldsymbol{b}^{(i)}(\boldsymbol{x})\, \phi_A(\boldsymbol{x}), \qquad \boldsymbol{M}(\boldsymbol{x})\, \boldsymbol{b}^{(i)}(\boldsymbol{x}) = \boldsymbol{H}^{(i)}_0, \]
with the same moment matrix \( \boldsymbol{M} \). The right-hand side \( \boldsymbol{H}^{(i)}_0 = -\partial \boldsymbol{H}(\boldsymbol{z}) / \partial z_i |_{\boldsymbol{z} = \boldsymbol{0}} \) (the sign stems from the shifted argument \( \boldsymbol{z} = \boldsymbol{x} - \boldsymbol{x}_A \)) is \( -1 \) at the position of the linear monomial \( z_i \) in \( \boldsymbol{H} \) and zero elsewhere (the position is found from Math::computeMonomialBasisGradient() at the origin, so it is correct for any order). This enforces the gradient reproducing conditions
\[ \sum_A \Psi^{(i)}_A(\boldsymbol{x})\, \boldsymbol{x}_A^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} = \frac{\partial \boldsymbol{x}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}{\partial x_i}, \qquad |\boldsymbol{\alpha}| \le n, \]
so the implicit gradients reproduce the derivatives of all polynomials up to the completeness order, but they are not the derivatives of the values \( \Psi_A \). They require neither kernel gradients nor \( \boldsymbol{M}_{,i} \): all \( 1 + d \) right-hand sides are solved with one QR decomposition of \( \boldsymbol{M} \).

B-spline basis functions (Marmot::Math):

B-spline basis functions of arbitrary degree and their derivatives (Cox–de Boor recursion).

The functions evaluate the \( i \)-th B-spline basis function \( N_{i,p}(u) \) of degree \( p \) over a non-decreasing knot vector \( \{z_0, z_1, \dots\} \), and its derivative \( \mathrm{d}N_{i,p}/\mathrm{d}u \). The degree is a template parameter, so the recursion is resolved at compile time. They are declared in the global namespace.
