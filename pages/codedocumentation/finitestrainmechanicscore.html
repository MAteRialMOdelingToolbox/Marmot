class GradientEnhancedHughesWingetWrapper : public Marmot::Materials::MaterialGradientEnhancedFiniteStrain
#include <MarmotMaterialGradientEnhancedHughesWinget.h>

Inheritance diagram for Marmot::Materials::GradientEnhancedHughesWingetWrapper:

Collaboration diagram for Marmot::Materials::GradientEnhancedHughesWingetWrapper:

Decorator presenting a small-strain gradient-enhanced material as a finite-strain one.

The gradient-enhanced sibling of HughesWingetWrapper, and it works the same way: the wrapped material is driven by the objective strain increment of the Hughes-Winget algorithm, evaluated on the mid-step configuration,

\[ \Delta\boldsymbol{l} = \left(\boldsymbol{F}^{(n+1)} - \boldsymbol{F}^{(n)}\right) \boldsymbol{F}_{\text{mid}}^{-1}, \qquad \boldsymbol{F}_{\text{mid}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(\boldsymbol{F}^{(n)} + \boldsymbol{F}^{(n+1)}\right), \]
split into \( \Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \) and \( \Delta\boldsymbol{\Omega} \), with the stress of the previous increment rotated forward by the Cayley transform of \( \Delta\boldsymbol{\Omega} \) and the resulting Cauchy stress pushed to the Kirchhoff stress \( \boldsymbol{\tau} = J\,\boldsymbol{\sigma} \).

On top of that it carries the nonlocal field across the two interfaces:

this interface reports

from the wrapped material

response.L

KLocal(0), a total, which is what both sides mean

response.nonLocalRadius

\( \sqrt{c(0)} \)

tangents.dTau_dN

\( J\,\partial\boldsymbol{\sigma}/\partial\bar{N} \)

tangents.dL_dF

\( \partial K^{\text{local}}/\partial\Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \)

tangents.dL_dN

dKLocalddK(0,0)

dL_dF is chained through the Hughes-Winget kinematics. Each row is kept inside the 120-column limit deliberately: ReflowComments would otherwise wrap a longer one onto the next line and split the table in two.

Note

The wrapped material wants the nonlocal field and its increment, while this interface hands over only the total. The increment is therefore formed here, against a value of the last accepted increment carried in this wrapper’s own state (HughesWinget_N_n) – one slot more than the local sibling needs.

Note

The wrapped material must be incremental in stress. It has to update the Cauchy stress it is handed, not recompute one from a stored strain: the wrapper’s whole mechanism is to hand over the forward-rotated stress of the last increment, and a material that ignores that argument discards the rotation along with it. AT2PhaseField is a counter-example: it carries a six-component strain state (AT2PhaseField.h:60) and returns \( g(\varphi)\,\mathbb{C}:\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \) from it (AT2PhaseField.cpp:71), so its true \( \partial\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(n+1)}/\partial\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\text{rot}} \) is zero rather than the identity. Measured: wrapped, a rigid rotation on top of an anisotropic stretch leaves its Kirchhoff stress bit-for-bit unrotated. It is therefore deliberately not registered with this wrapper, and a total-strain-based model must not be either.

Note

Only the stress is rotated. Tensor-valued internal variables of the wrapped material are passed through untouched and are therefore not objective under large incremental rotations. A model whose internal state is entirely scalar is unaffected.

Note

The interaction \( c \) is reported through its square root, and its derivative \( \partial c/\partial\bar{N} \) cannot be forwarded at all: the finite-strain interface has no slot for it. Harmless for a material whose \( c \) is constant (AT2PhaseField is); a material built on MarmotDecreasingInteractions would keep a correct residual but lose that term of its consistent tangent.

Note

The nonlocal balance is formulated in the material configuration by the consumers of this interface, so \( c \) is handed over as the material constant the wrapped model reports, neither pushed forward nor weighted by \( J \).

class HughesWingetWrapper : public Marmot::Materials::MaterialFiniteStrain

Inheritance diagram for Marmot::Materials::HughesWingetWrapper:

Collaboration diagram for Marmot::Materials::HughesWingetWrapper:

Decorator presenting a small-strain (hypoelastic) material as a finite-strain material.

The wrapped material is driven by the objective strain increment of the Hughes-Winget algorithm, evaluated on the mid-step configuration,

\[ \Delta\boldsymbol{l} = \left(\boldsymbol{F}^{(n+1)} - \boldsymbol{F}^{(n)}\right) \boldsymbol{F}_{\text{mid}}^{-1}, \qquad \boldsymbol{F}_{\text{mid}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(\boldsymbol{F}^{(n)} + \boldsymbol{F}^{(n+1)}\right), \]
split into its symmetric and skew parts \( \Delta\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \) and \( \Delta\boldsymbol{\Omega} \). The incremental rotation follows from the Cayley transform
\[ \Delta\boldsymbol{R} = \left(\boldsymbol{I} - \tfrac{1}{2}\Delta\boldsymbol{\Omega}\right)^{-1} \left(\boldsymbol{I} + \tfrac{1}{2}\Delta\boldsymbol{\Omega}\right), \]
which is orthogonal to machine precision. The stress carried over from the previous increment is rotated forward, \( \boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\text{rot}} = \Delta\boldsymbol{R}\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(n)} \Delta\boldsymbol{R}^{T} \), the wrapped material is evaluated, and the resulting Cauchy stress is pushed to the Kirchhoff stress \( \boldsymbol{\tau} = J\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(n+1)} \) expected by the finite-strain consumers.

Note

Only the stress is rotated. Tensor-valued internal variables of the wrapped material (kinematic hardening back stresses, plastic strain tensors, anisotropic damage tensors) are passed through untouched and are therefore not objective under large incremental rotations. Models whose internal state is scalar or isotropic-invariant are unaffected.

Note

The characteristic element length is not derived from any geometry here. It must be assigned explicitly via setCharacteristicElementLength; until then it is NaN, so a wrapped material that relies on it fails loudly instead of silently regularising incorrectly.

Inheritance diagram for Marmot::Materials::MaterialGradientEnhancedFiniteStrain:

Abstract base class for gradient-enhanced (implicit-gradient) materials in the finite strain regime.

The finite-strain counterpart of Marmot::Materials::MaterialGradientEnhancedHypoElastic: the displacement field is coupled to a single scalar nonlocal field \( \bar{N} \) governed by the additional balance equation

\[ \bar{N} - c\,\nabla^2\bar{N} = L(\boldsymbol{F},\,\bar{N}), \qquad c = R^2, \]
where \( L \) is the local driving force reported in ConstitutiveResponse::L and \( R \) the nonlocal radius reported in ConstitutiveResponse::nonLocalRadius.

Note

The nonlocal balance is formulated in the material (reference) configuration. Consumers take the gradients with respect to \( \boldsymbol{X} \) and integrate over the reference volume, so \( c \) is a material constant and is neither pushed forward nor weighted by \( J \). The stress, in contrast, is the Kirchhoff stress \( \boldsymbol{\tau} = J\,\boldsymbol{\sigma} \), which is what integrating against the reference volume requires.

Note

The interface carries a single scalar nonlocal field, unlike the small-strain Marmot::Materials::MaterialGradientEnhancedHypoElastic, which is templated on the number of nonlocal variables. It also has **no slot for \( \partial c/\partial\bar{N} \)**: a material with damage-dependent interactions (see MarmotDecreasingInteractions) can report the current \( c \), but not its derivative, so its consistent tangent would be incomplete.

Note

Member names, struct shapes and call signatures are fixed by the consumers of this interface (GradientEnhancedFiniteStrainMaterialPoint / …Particle / …DisplacementElement), including the six-argument ConstitutiveResponse constructor.

Computes the incremental plastic deformation gradient from the plastic velocity gradient using the exponential map, evaluated by scaling and squaring.

The result is that of exponentialMap(), but the series is only evaluated for \( \Delta\boldsymbol{G}^p / 2^s \) with \( |\Delta\boldsymbol{G}^p| / 2^s \leq 1/2 \), and then squared \( s \) times,

enum class HughesWingetTangent

Selects how the algorithmic tangent \( \partial\boldsymbol{\tau}/\partial\boldsymbol{F} \) of Marmot::Materials::HughesWingetWrapper is evaluated.

Values:

enumerator Analytic

Closed-form linearisation of the Hughes-Winget update, assuming \( \partial\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(n+1)}/\partial\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\text{rot}} = \boldsymbol{I} \). Exact while the wrapped material responds elastically. One wrapped-material evaluation.

enumerator Exact

As HughesWingetTangent::Analytic, but the operator \( \boldsymbol{S} = \partial\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(n+1)}/\partial\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\text{rot}} \) is recovered by forward-differencing the wrapped material with respect to its six incoming stress components. Fully consistent. Seven wrapped-material evaluations.

enumerator Numerical

The complete update is forward-differenced with respect to the nine components of \( \boldsymbol{F} \). Fully consistent and model-agnostic, but the most expensive option (eleven wrapped-material evaluations). Intended for verification and debugging.
